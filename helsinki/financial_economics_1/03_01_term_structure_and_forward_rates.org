* Term Structure of Interest Rates

** The Spot Rate Model
In reality, interest rates depend strongly on the *maturity* of the investment (the investment horizon).
Let $\Delta > 0$ be the base period length.
At time $0$, the market offers spot interest rates $s_1, s_2, \dots, s_T$ for investments of durations $\Delta, 2\Delta, \dots, T\Delta$:

#+caption: Table 3.1: The Term Structure (Spot Rates) at Time 0
| Maturity | $\Delta$ | $2\Delta$ | $3\Delta$ | $\dots$ | $T\Delta$ |
|----------+----------+-----------+-----------+---------+-----------|
| Spot Rate| $s_1$    | $s_2$     | $s_3$     | $\dots$ | $s_T$     |

A capital deposit of amount $C$ deposited at time $0$ with maturity $j\Delta$ matures at time $j\Delta$ to:
$$ C (1 + s_j)^{j\Delta} \tag{3.1} $$
Here, $s_j$ is the annualized *spot interest rate* for maturity $j\Delta$. By convention, $s_0 = 0$.

** Zero-Coupon Bonds
A *zero-coupon bond (ZCB)* of maturity $j\Delta$ and face value (nominal value) $1$ pays exactly $1$ unit of currency at time $j\Delta$ and zero at all other times.
By Formula (3.1), the unique arbitrage-free price of a $j$-period zero-coupon bond at time $0$ is:
$$ P(0, j\Delta) = (1 + s_j)^{-j\Delta} \tag{3.8} $$
Zero-coupon bonds serve as the fundamental building blocks of fixed-income financial markets.

* Forward Interest Rates

** Definition and Derivation
#+begin_quote
*Definition 3.1 (Forward Interest Rate):*
The *forward interest rate* $f_j$ is the annualized interest rate contracted at time $0$ for a loan or deposit taking place over the future time period $[(j-1)\Delta, j\Delta)$.
#+end_quote

Consider two alternative ways to invest capital $C$ from time $0$ to time $j\Delta$:
1. *Strategy A (Long-term spot deposit):*
   Invest $C$ directly for $j$ periods at spot rate $s_j$. Wealth at time $j\Delta$ is:
   $$ C (1 + s_j)^{j\Delta} $$
2. *Strategy B (Roll-over via forward contract):*
   Invest $C$ for $j-1$ periods at spot rate $s_{j-1}$, and simultaneously lock in a forward contract to reinvest the proceeds from time $(j-1)\Delta$ to time $j\Delta$ at forward rate $f_j$. Wealth at time $j\Delta$ is:
   $$ C (1 + s_{j-1})^{(j-1)\Delta} (1 + f_j)^\Delta $$

Under the assumption of *no arbitrage*, both strategies must produce identical wealth at time $j\Delta$:
$$ (1 + s_j)^{j\Delta} = (1 + s_{j-1})^{(j-1)\Delta} (1 + f_j)^\Delta \tag{3.2} $$

Solving for the forward rate $(1 + f_j)^\Delta$ gives:
$$ (1 + f_j)^\Delta = \frac{(1 + s_j)^{j\Delta}}{(1 + s_{j-1})^{(j-1)\Delta}} \tag{3.3} $$
or equivalently:
$$ f_j = \left( \frac{(1 + s_j)^{j\Delta}}{(1 + s_{j-1})^{(j-1)\Delta}} \right)^{1/\Delta} - 1 \tag{3.4} $$

By chaining Equation (3.2) iteratively, we obtain the fundamental decomposition of the spot compounding factor as the product of forward compounding factors:
$$ (1 + s_k)^{k\Delta} = \prod_{j=1}^k (1 + f_j)^\Delta \tag{3.5} $$

* Valuation of Cash Flows under the Term Structure

** Theorem 3.1 (Arbitrage-Free Price of a Cash Flow under Spot Rates)
#+begin_quote
*Theorem 3.1:*
In the term structure market described by Table 3.1, the unique arbitrage-free price at time $0$ of a deterministic cash flow $B(\Delta), B(2\Delta), \dots, B(T\Delta)$ is:
$$ V(0) = \sum_{j=1}^T (1 + s_j)^{-j\Delta} B(j\Delta) = \sum_{j=1}^T \left[ \prod_{k=1}^j (1 + f_k) \right]^{-\Delta} B(j\Delta) \tag{3.7} $$
#+end_quote

*** Proof
Each individual payment $B(j\Delta)$ can be replicated at time $0$ by purchasing $B(j\Delta)$ units of a zero-coupon bond maturing at $j\Delta$, costing $B(j\Delta)(1+s_j)^{-j\Delta}$.
By the linearity of pricing (no arbitrage), the total cost to replicate the entire cash stream is the sum of the individual discounted payments. Substituting $(1+s_j)^{-j\Delta} = \prod_{k=1}^j (1+f_k)^{-\Delta}$ from Equation (3.5) yields Equation (3.7). $\blacksquare$

* Absence of Arbitrage in the Term Structure

** Non-Negativity of Forward Rates
#+begin_quote
*Proposition 3.1 (Arbitrage-Free Term Structure Condition):*
In an economy where holding cash is costless (cash has a zero nominal return), the market is free of arbitrage if and only if all forward rates are non-negative:
$$ f_j \ge 0 \quad \text{for all } j = 1, 2, \dots, T $$
Equivalently, the accumulated return factors must be non-decreasing:
$$ (1 + s_k)^{k\Delta} \ge (1 + s_j)^{j\Delta} \quad \text{for all } k \ge j \ge 1 $$
#+end_quote

*** Arbitrage Strategy when $f_j < 0$
Suppose for some $j \ge 2$, the forward rate is strictly negative: $f_j < 0$.
Then $(1 + f_j)^\Delta < 1$, which implies:
$$ (1 + s_j)^{j\Delta} < (1 + s_{j-1})^{(j-1)\Delta} $$
An arbitrageur exploits this mispricing at time $0$ as follows:
1. *Borrow for $j$ periods:* Borrow $C$ at spot rate $s_j$.
   At time $j\Delta$, the repayment obligation will be:
   $$ C (1 + s_j)^{j\Delta} $$
2. *Lend for $j-1$ periods:* Deposit the borrowed $C$ at spot rate $s_{j-1}$.
   At time $(j-1)\Delta$, the deposit yields:
   $$ C (1 + s_{j-1})^{(j-1)\Delta} $$
3. *Hold as physical cash:* From time $(j-1)\Delta$ to time $j\Delta$, hold this money in cash (earning $0\%$ nominal interest).
4. *Payoff at time $j\Delta$:*
   The investor has cash $C (1 + s_{j-1})^{(j-1)\Delta}$ and owes $C (1 + s_j)^{j\Delta}$.
   The net profit at time $j\Delta$ is:
   $$ C \left[ (1 + s_{j-1})^{(j-1)\Delta} - (1 + s_j)^{j\Delta} \right] > 0 $$
Zero cash was needed at time $0$, no liabilities remain after time $j\Delta$, and a guaranteed positive profit was made.
Thus, $f_j < 0$ violates absence of arbitrage. $\blacksquare$

* Pricing Coupon Bonds and Yield to Maturity

** Coupon Bond Pricing
A coupon-bearing bond with face value $F$, coupon rate $c$, maturity $T\Delta$, and periodic coupon payment $C = c F \Delta$ generates the cash flow:
$$ B(j\Delta) = C \quad (j = 1, \dots, T-1), \quad B(T\Delta) = C + F $$
By Theorem 3.1, its arbitrage-free price $P$ is:
$$ P = \sum_{j=1}^T C (1 + s_j)^{-j\Delta} + F (1 + s_T)^{-T\Delta} \tag{3.13} $$

** Yield to Maturity (YTM)
The *yield to maturity* $y$ is the single internal rate of return that equates the bond's market price $P$ to the present value of its future cash flows:
$$ P = \sum_{j=1}^T C (1 + y)^{-j\Delta} + F (1 + y)^{-T\Delta} \tag{3.14} $$
- If $y = s_j$ for all $j$ (flat yield curve), the bond price under YTM matches Theorem 3.1.
- In general, YTM is a complex weighted average of spot rates along the term structure.
