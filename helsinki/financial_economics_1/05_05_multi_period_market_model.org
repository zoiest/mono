* Mathematical Setup of the Multi-Period Market

** Information Structure and Filtrations
Let $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a probability space, and let $t \in \{0, 1, \dots, T\}$ denote discrete trading dates.
Information revelation over time is modeled by a *filtration* $\mathbb{F} = (\mathcal{F}_t)_{t=0}^T$:
$$ \{\emptyset, \Omega\} = \mathcal{F}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{F}_1 \subseteq \dots \subseteq \mathcal{F}_T = \mathcal{F} $$
In a finite discrete state space, each \(\sigma\)-algebra $\mathcal{F}_t$ is generated by a partition $\mathcal{P}_t$ of $\Omega$ that becomes finer as $t$ increases (tree structure).

** Price Processes and Predictable Strategies
- *Price Processes:* The prices of $N$ securities are given by an \(\mathbb{R}^N\)-valued stochastic process:
  $$ S(t) = (S_1(t), S_2(t), \dots, S_N(t))^T, \quad t = 0, 1, \dots, T $$
  which is *adapted* to $\mathbb{F}$ (i.e. $S(t)$ is \(\mathcal{F}_t\)-measurable).
- *Trading Strategies:* A trading strategy is an \(\mathbb{R}^N\)-valued process:
  $$ \theta(t) = (\theta_1(t), \dots, \theta_N(t))^T, \quad t = 1, \dots, T $$
  where $\theta_n(t)$ denotes the number of units held in asset $n$ during the interval $(t-1, t]$.
  Crucially, $\theta(t)$ is *predictable* (\(\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\)-measurable): the portfolio held between $t-1$ and $t$ must be selected using only the information available up to time $t-1$.

** Portfolio Value and the Self-Financing Condition
- The *value process* $V(t)$ generated by strategy $\theta$ is:
  $$ V(t) = S(t) \theta(t) = \sum_{n=1}^N \theta_n(t) S_n(t) $$

#+begin_quote
*Definition (Self-Financing Strategy):*
A trading strategy $\theta$ is *self-financing* if at each portfolio rebalancing time $t \in \{1, \dots, T-1\}$, no capital is injected or withdrawn:
$$ S(t) \theta(t+1) = S(t) \theta(t) \tag{5.16} $$
The value of the new portfolio $\theta(t+1)$ chosen at time $t$ exactly equals the liquidated value of the previous portfolio $\theta(t)$.
#+end_quote

Under the self-financing condition, wealth increments are driven entirely by asset price movements:
$$ \Delta V(t) = V(t) - V(t-1) = \theta(t) \cdot \Delta S(t) = \sum_{n=1}^N \theta_n(t) [S_n(t) - S_n(t-1)] $$

** Discounted Price Processes
Choose Security 1 as the *numeraire* (e.g., a risk-free bank account or zero-coupon bond, $S_1(t) > 0$).
Define discounted prices and discounted wealth:
$$ S^*(t) = \frac{S(t)}{S_1(t)}, \quad V^*(t) = \frac{V(t)}{S_1(t)} $$
For any self-financing strategy $\theta$:
$$ V^*(t) = V^*(0) + \sum_{k=1}^t \theta(k) \cdot \Delta S^*(k) \tag{5.17} $$

* Multi-Period Fundamental Theorems of Asset Pricing

** Definition 5.9 (Multi-Period Arbitrage)
#+begin_quote
*Definition 5.9 (Arbitrage Opportunity):*
A self-financing strategy $\theta$ is an *arbitrage opportunity* if:
1. $V(0) \le 0$,
2. $V(T) \ge 0$ almost surely, and
3. $\mathbb{P}(V(T) > 0) > 0$ (or $V(0) < 0$).
#+end_quote

** Theorem 5.10 (Multi-Period FTAP 1)
#+begin_quote
*Theorem 5.10 (First Fundamental Theorem in Multi-Period Markets):*
The multi-period market is *arbitrage-free* if and only if there exists a probability measure $\mathbb{Q} \sim \mathbb{P}$ (an equivalent martingale measure) under which all discounted asset price processes $S^*(t)$ are \((\mathbb{Q}, \mathbb{F})\)-martingales:
$$ \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[S^*(t+1) \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = S^*(t) \quad \text{for all } t = 0, 1, \dots, T-1 \tag{5.18} $$
#+end_quote

*** Proof Idea via Dynamic Induction
By backward induction, a multi-period tree model can be decomposed into a collection of single-period submodels $M_0, M_1, \dots, M_r$ at each branch node.
- Lemma 5.11: The multi-period model is arbitrage-free if and only if every single-period conditional submodel $M_j$ is arbitrage-free.
- Lemma 5.12: A global martingale measure $\mathbb{Q}$ is constructed by multiplying conditional transition probabilities $q(node \to child)$ obtained from the single-period FTAP 1 (Theorem 5.3). $\blacksquare$

** Theorem 5.14 (Multi-Period FTAP 2)
#+begin_quote
*Theorem 5.14 (Second Fundamental Theorem in Multi-Period Markets):*
An arbitrage-free multi-period market is *complete* if and only if the equivalent martingale measure $\mathbb{Q}$ is *unique*.
#+end_quote

* The Binomial Tree Model (Cox-Ross-Rubinstein)

The classic example of a complete multi-period market is the *Cox-Ross-Rubinstein (CRR) Binomial Model*:
- Security 1 (Bond): $S_1(t) = (1+r)^t$.
- Security 2 (Stock): At each step, $S_2(t+1) = u S_2(t)$ (up-step) or $S_2(t+1) = d S_2(t)$ (down-step), with $0 < d < 1+r < u$.

** Arbitrage Freedom and Unique Risk-Neutral Measure
At each node, the martingale equation requires:
$$ S_2^*(t) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[S_2^*(t+1) \mid \mathcal{F}_t] \implies S_2(t) = \frac{1}{1+r} [q u S_2(t) + (1-q) d S_2(t)] $$
Dividing by $S_2(t)$ gives:
$$ 1+r = q u + (1-q) d \implies q = \frac{1+r-d}{u-d} $$
Since $d < 1+r < u$, the risk-neutral probability $q \in (0, 1)$ exists and is *strictly unique*.
By Theorem 5.14, *the binomial model is completely arbitrage-free and complete.*

** Option Pricing by Backward Induction
For a European claim $X = g(S(T))$ at maturity $T$, the price $V_t$ at any node is computed by backwards recursion:
$$ V_t = \frac{1}{1+r} \left[ q V_{t+1}^u + (1-q) V_{t+1}^d \right] $$
with replicating stock holding $\theta_2(t+1) = \frac{V_{t+1}^u - V_{t+1}^d}{S(t)(u - d)}$ (\(\Delta\)-hedging).
