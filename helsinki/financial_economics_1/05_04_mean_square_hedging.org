* Motivation: Hedging in Incomplete Markets

In an incomplete market ($M > N$), a general contingent claim $X$ cannot be perfectly replicated:
$$ X \notin \operatorname{Im}(S(1)) = \{S(1)\theta \mid \theta \in \mathbb{R}^N\} $$
An agent who has written or purchased claim $X$ faces *unavoidable intrinsic risk*.
Under such conditions, the standard approach is to choose a hedging portfolio $\theta$ that minimizes a penalty function of the tracking error.
The most tractable and widely studied objective is *Mean-Square Error (MSE) minimization* (quadratic loss).

---

* The One-Period Quadratic Hedging Problem

** Formulation
Let $X \in L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ be a target claim payable at time $1$.
The investor solves:
$$ \min_{V(0) \in \mathbb{R}, \, \theta \in \mathbb{R}^N} \mathbb{E}\left[ (X - S(1)\theta)^2 \right] \quad \text{subject to} \quad S(0)\theta = V(0) \tag{5.11} $$

** Decomposition into Risk-Free and Risky Assets
Suppose Security 1 is a risk-free bank deposit with interest rate $r$:
$$ S_1(0) = 1, \quad S_1(1) = 1 + r $$
Let $\theta = (\theta_1, \theta')^T$, where $\theta' = (\theta_2, \dots, \theta_N)^T \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}$ denotes the holdings in the $N-1$ risky assets.
The budget constraint gives:
$$ V(0) = \theta_1 + \sum_{n=2}^N \theta_n S_n(0) \implies \theta_1 = V(0) - \sum_{n=2}^N \theta_n S_n(0) $$

Substituting $\theta_1$ into the terminal wealth:
$$ S(1)\theta = \theta_1 (1 + r) + \sum_{n=2}^N \theta_n S_n(1) = (1 + r)V(0) + \sum_{n=2}^N \theta_n \left[ S_n(1) - (1 + r)S_n(0) \right] $$
Define the *excess return vector* of the risky assets:
$$ \Delta S' = \begin{pmatrix} S_2(1) - (1 + r)S_2(0) \\ \vdots \\ S_N(1) - (1 + r)S_N(0) \end{pmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1} $$

The optimization problem (5.11) reduces to:
$$ \min_{V(0) \in \mathbb{R}, \, \theta' \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}} \mathbb{E}\left[ \left( X - (1 + r)V(0) - \theta'^T \Delta S' \right)^2 \right] \tag{5.14} $$

---

* Optimal Hedging Solution and Orthogonal Projection

Let:
- $\Sigma = \operatorname{Cov}(\Delta S') \in \mathbb{R}^{(N-1) \times (N-1)}$ be the covariance matrix of excess returns (assumed positive definite).
- $\Gamma = \operatorname{Cov}(X, \Delta S') \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}$ be the covariance vector between the claim $X$ and the risky asset excess returns.
- $\mu = \mathbb{E}[\Delta S'] \in \mathbb{R}^{N-1}$ be the expected excess return vector.

Decomposing the mean square error into variance and squared bias:
$$ \mathbb{E}[(Y - c)^2] = \operatorname{Var}(Y) + (\mathbb{E}[Y] - c)^2 $$
Setting $Y = X - \theta'^T \Delta S'$ and $c = (1 + r)V(0)$:
$$ \mathbb{E}\left[ (X - (1 + r)V(0) - \theta'^T \Delta S')^2 \right] = \operatorname{Var}(X - \theta'^T \Delta S') + \left( \mathbb{E}[X] - \theta'^T \mu - (1 + r)V(0) \right)^2 $$

To minimize this quantity:
1. *Optimal Initial Capital $V(0)$:*
   Choose $V(0)$ to eliminate the squared bias term completely:
   $$ (1 + r)V(0) = \mathbb{E}[X] - \theta'^T \mu \implies V(0) = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X] - \theta'^T \mathbb{E}[\Delta S']}{1 + r} \tag{5.15} $$
2. *Optimal Risky Portfolio $\theta'$:*
   Minimize the variance:
   $$ \min_{\theta'} \operatorname{Var}(X - \theta'^T \Delta S') = \operatorname{Var}(X) - 2 \theta'^T \Gamma + \theta'^T \Sigma \theta' $$
   Setting the gradient to zero:
   $$ -2\Gamma + 2\Sigma \theta' = 0 \implies \theta' = \Sigma^{-1} \Gamma $$

#+begin_quote
*Theorem 5.5 (Optimal Mean-Square Hedging Strategy):*
The unique optimal mean-square error hedging portfolio is:
$$ \theta' = \Sigma^{-1} \operatorname{Cov}(X, \Delta S') $$
with optimal initial capital:
$$ V(0) = \frac{1}{1 + r} \left( \mathbb{E}[X] - \theta'^T \mathbb{E}[\Delta S'] \right) $$
The minimal residual unhedgeable risk is:
$$ \operatorname{Var}(X - S(1)\theta) = \operatorname{Var}(X) - \Gamma^T \Sigma^{-1} \Gamma $$
#+end_quote

This is the classic projection of $X$ onto the subspace spanned by the constant and the asset returns in the Hilbert space $L^2(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$.
