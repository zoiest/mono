* Asset-Liability Management and Interest Rate Risk

** Motivation: Pension Funds and Insurance Companies
Consider a financial institution (such as a life insurance company or pension fund) that holds:
- *Inflows (Assets):* A cash flow of asset payments $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_T \ge 0$ generated by bonds and loans.
- *Outflows (Liabilities):* A pre-committed stream of contractual liabilities $L_1, L_2, \dots, L_T \ge 0$ (e.g., guaranteed pension payouts).

Assuming a flat interest rate $s > 0$, the net surplus value at time $0$ is:
$$ f(s) = V_A(s) - V_L(s) = \sum_{j=1}^T (A_j - L_j)(1 + s)^{-j} \tag{3.15} $$

If interest rates change unexpectedly from $s$ to $s + \Delta s$, the net surplus $f(s)$ fluctuates, potentially leaving the institution insolvent ($f(s + \Delta s) < 0$).
*Immunization* is the process of structuring the asset portfolio such that the institution's net surplus remains protected against adverse interest rate shifts.

---

* The Hierarchy of Immunization Strategies

** 1. Exact Cash Flow Matching (Dedication)
The strictest form of protection sets:
$$ A_j = L_j \quad \text{for every } j = 1, \dots, T $$
Under cash flow matching, $f(s) \equiv 0$ identically for all interest rate levels $s$.
However, cash flow matching is often impractical or excessively expensive due to market liquidity limitations.

** 2. Present Value Matching
At the current market interest rate $s_0$:
$$ f(s_0) = 0 \iff V_A(s_0) = V_L(s_0) $$
This ensures solvency today, but offers zero stability if $s$ shifts.

** 3. Duration Matching (First-Order Immunization)
To make the net surplus insensitive to infinitesimal shifts around $s_0$, we require the first derivative to vanish:
$$ f'(s_0) = 0 \tag{3.16} $$

Computing the derivative:
$$ f'(s) = -\sum_{j=1}^T j A_j (1 + s)^{-j-1} + \sum_{j=1}^T j L_j (1 + s)^{-j-1} = 0 $$
Multiplying by $-(1+s)$ yields:
$$ \sum_{j=1}^T j A_j (1 + s)^{-j} = \sum_{j=1}^T j L_j (1 + s)^{-j} $$

* Duration and Convexity

** Macaulay Duration
#+begin_quote
*Definition 3.2 (Macaulay Duration):*
For a cash flow $A_1, \dots, A_T$ with present value $V(s) = \sum_{j=1}^T A_j (1+s)^{-j}$, its *Macaulay duration* $D(s)$ is the weighted average maturity of payments:
$$ D(s) = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^T j A_j (1 + s)^{-j}}{\sum_{j=1}^T A_j (1 + s)^{-j}} = \sum_{j=1}^T j \cdot w_j(s) \tag{3.17} $$
where $w_j(s) = \frac{A_j (1+s)^{-j}}{V(s)}$ represents the fraction of total present value contributed by payment $j$. Note that $\sum_{j=1}^T w_j(s) = 1$.
#+end_quote

** Modified Duration
The *modified duration* $D^*(s)$ measures the percentage price sensitivity with respect to a change in the interest rate:
$$ D^*(s) = \frac{D(s)}{1 + s} = -\frac{1}{V(s)} \frac{dV(s)}{ds} $$
Hence, the first-order Taylor approximation for price change is:
$$ \frac{\Delta V}{V} \approx -D^*(s) \Delta s $$

** First-Order Condition via Duration
When $V_A(s) = V_L(s)$, the condition $f'(s) = 0$ is equivalent to:
$$ D_A(s) = D_L(s) $$
*The Macaulay duration of assets must equal the Macaulay duration of liabilities.*

---

* Redington Immunization (Second-Order Immunization)

** Taylor Expansion of the Net Surplus
Expanding $f(s)$ in a second-order Taylor series around $s_0$:
$$ f(s_0 + \Delta s) \approx f(s_0) + f'(s_0) \Delta s + \frac{1}{2} f''(s_0) (\Delta s)^2 $$
If:
1. $f(s_0) = 0$ (Value matching),
2. $f'(s_0) = 0$ (Duration matching),
then:
$$ f(s_0 + \Delta s) \approx \frac{1}{2} f''(s_0) (\Delta s)^2 $$

To guarantee that any interest rate movement (up or down, $\Delta s \ne 0$) creates a strictly positive surplus ($f(s_0 + \Delta s) > 0$), we must require:
$$ f''(s_0) > 0 $$

** Convexity
#+begin_quote
*Definition 3.3 (Convexity):*
The *convexity* $C(s)$ of a cash flow is the normalized second derivative of its present value:
$$ C(s) = \frac{1}{V(s)} \frac{d^2 V(s)}{ds^2} = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^T j(j+1) A_j (1 + s)^{-j-2}}{V(s)} $$
#+end_quote

** Redington's Theorem
#+begin_quote
*Theorem 3.2 (Redington Immunization):*
A portfolio of assets and liabilities is immunized against small shifts in interest rates if the following three conditions simultaneously hold at market rate $s_0$:
1. *Present value balance:* $V_A(s_0) = V_L(s_0)$
2. *Duration matching:* $D_A(s_0) = D_L(s_0)$
3. *Convexity condition:* $C_A(s_0) > C_L(s_0)$ (or equivalently $f''(s_0) > 0$)
#+end_quote

Under these conditions, $s_0$ is a strict local minimum of $f(s)$. Any non-zero interest rate shock $\Delta s \ne 0$ increases the asset value relative to liabilities.

* Mathematical Properties of Duration

** Proposition: Duration is Strictly Decreasing in $s$
Let $A_1, \dots, A_T > 0$ and $s > 0$. Denote:
$$ g(s) = \sum_{j=1}^T A_j (1 + s)^{-j}, \quad M(s) = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^T j^2 A_j (1 + s)^{-j}}{g(s)} $$
Notice that:
- $D(s) = \mathbb{E}_w[J]$, where random variable $J$ takes value $j$ with probability $w_j = \frac{A_j(1+s)^{-j}}{g(s)}$.
- $M(s) = \mathbb{E}_w[J^2]$.

By Jensen's inequality or the non-negativity of variance:
$$ \operatorname{Var}_w(J) = \mathbb{E}_w[J^2] - (\mathbb{E}_w[J])^2 = M(s) - D(s)^2 \ge 0 \implies M(s) \ge D(s)^2 $$

Now compute the derivative of $D(s)$ with respect to $s$:
$$ \frac{d D(s)}{ds} = \frac{d}{ds} \left[ \frac{\sum_{j=1}^T j A_j (1+s)^{-j}}{g(s)} \right] $$
Using the quotient rule:
$$ \frac{d D(s)}{ds} = \frac{-\sum_{j=1}^T j^2 A_j (1+s)^{-j-1} \cdot g(s) - \left( \sum_{j=1}^T j A_j (1+s)^{-j} \right) \left( -\sum_{j=1}^T j A_j (1+s)^{-j-1} \right)}{g(s)^2} $$
Factoring out $-(1+s)^{-1}$:
$$ \frac{d D(s)}{ds} = -\frac{1}{1+s} \left[ \frac{\sum_{j=1}^T j^2 A_j (1+s)^{-j}}{g(s)} - \left( \frac{\sum_{j=1}^T j A_j (1+s)^{-j}}{g(s)} \right)^2 \right] = -\frac{1}{1+s} \left[ M(s) - D(s)^2 \right] $$
Since $\operatorname{Var}_w(J) = M(s) - D(s)^2 > 0$ for any non-degenerate cash flow ($T \ge 2$), we conclude:
$$ \frac{d D(s)}{ds} < 0 \quad \text{for all } s > 0 $$
*Duration is a strictly decreasing function of the interest rate.*
