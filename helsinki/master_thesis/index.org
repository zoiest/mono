* Mathematical Framework

** 1. Classical Asset Dynamics & Pricing PDE

*** The Black-Scholes-Merton Benchmark
Under the risk-neutral measure $\mathbb{Q}$, the asset price $S_t$ satisfies:
$$ dS_t = r S_t \, dt + \sigma S_t \, dW_t^\mathbb{Q}, $$
where $r$ is the risk-free rate, $\sigma > 0$ is constant volatility, and $W_t^\mathbb{Q}$ is a standard Brownian motion.

*** The Heston Stochastic Volatility Model
To capture market volatility smiles and skews, the asset variance $v_t = \sigma_t^2$ is modeled as a mean-reverting Cox-Ingersoll-Ross (CIR) square-root diffusion:
$$ dS_t = r S_t \, dt + \sqrt{v_t} S_t \, dW_{1, t}^\mathbb{Q}, $$
$$ dv_t = \kappa (\theta - v_t) \, dt + \xi \sqrt{v_t} \, dW_{2, t}^\mathbb{Q}, $$
with correlated Brownian motions $d\langle W_1^\mathbb{Q}, W_2^\mathbb{Q} \rangle_t = \rho \, dt$, where:
- $\kappa > 0$ is the mean-reversion speed,
- $\theta > 0$ is the long-term variance,
- $\xi > 0$ is the volatility of variance (vol-of-vol),
- $\rho \in [-1, 1]$ is the asset-volatility correlation (typically $\rho < 0$ for equity leverage effects),
- The Feller condition $2\kappa\theta > \xi^2$ guarantees that the variance process $v_t$ remains strictly positive almost surely.

** 2. Target Derivative Contracts & Payoffs

The thesis systematically investigates three classes of European-style contingent claims:
1. *Vanilla European Call/Put:*
   $$ \Phi_{\text{vanilla}}(S_T) = \max(S_T - K, 0). $$
2. *Path-Dependent Asian Option (Arithmetic Average):*
   $$ \Phi_{\text{Asian}}\left(\{S_{t_k}\}_{k=1}^M\right) = \max\left( \frac{1}{M}\sum_{k=1}^M S_{t_k} - K, \, 0 \right). $$
3. *Up-and-Out Barrier Option (Discontinuous Payoff):*
   $$ \Phi_{\text{barrier}}\left(\{S_{t_k}\}_{k=1}^M\right) = \max(S_T - K, 0) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{\max_{k} S_{t_k} < B\}}. $$

** 3. Sensitivity Risk Management (The Greeks)

Hedging requires computing functional derivatives of the discounted price $V_0 = e^{-rT}\mathbb{E}^\mathbb{Q}[\Phi(S)]$:
- *Delta:* $\Delta = \frac{\partial V_0}{\partial S_0}$
- *Gamma:* $\Gamma = \frac{\partial^2 V_0}{\partial S_0^2}$
- *Vega:* $\mathcal{V} = \frac{\partial V_0}{\partial \sigma}$ (or $\frac{\partial V_0}{\partial v_0}$)

*Methodological Comparison:*
1. *Finite Differences:* $\Delta \approx \frac{V(S_0 + \delta) - V(S_0 - \delta)}{2\delta}$. (Simple, but introduces high variance and step-size bias).
2. *Pathwise Derivative Method (Broadie & Glasserman):* Differentiating inside the expectation when the payoff is Lipschitz continuous:
   $$ \frac{\partial}{\partial S_0} \mathbb{E}^\mathbb{Q}[\Phi(S_T)] = \mathbb{E}^\mathbb{Q}\left[ \Phi'(S_T) \frac{\partial S_T}{\partial S_0} \right]. $$
3. *Malliavin Calculus Weights:* Differentiating via integration by parts on Wiener space, transforming derivatives of discontinuous payoffs (e.g. barrier options) into expectations with stochastic weights $\pi$:
   $$ \Delta = e^{-rT} \mathbb{E}^\mathbb{Q}[\Phi(S_T) \cdot \pi_\Delta], \quad \pi_\Delta = \frac{W_T^\mathbb{Q}}{S_0 \sigma T}. $$

** Quantum Advantage & Resource Profiling
- *Classical Monte Carlo Complexity:* To achieve precision $\varepsilon$, classical Monte Carlo requires $N = O(\varepsilon^{-2})$ sample trajectories (standard central limit theorem rate).
- *Quantum Amplitude Estimation Complexity:* IQAE achieves precision $\varepsilon$ using $M = O(\varepsilon^{-1})$ quantum oracle calls—a *quadratic speedup*.
- *The Practical Reality Check:*
  - Circuit depth grows proportionally to $O(\varepsilon^{-1})$.
  - Under NISQ decoherence and gate noise, deep Grover iterations accumulate error.
  - The thesis will establish the exact crossover point where quantum gate count surpasses classical GPU throughput under simulated device noise models.
