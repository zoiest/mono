** Working Title

*Nonlinear Option Pricing, Feedback-Adjusted Greeks, and Dynamic Hedging in an Agent-Based Market Simulation*
#+end_quote

** Core Motivation: Moving Beyond the Frictionless Paradigm
The classical Black-Scholes-Merton and continuous-time arbitrage theory (covered in /Financial Economics I/) rest on three idealized assumptions:
1. *Zero Market Impact (Price-Taking):* Individual hedging transactions do not perturb the underlying asset price ($dS_t$ is purely exogenous).
2. *Frictionless Trading:* Portfolios can be continuously rebalanced without bid-ask spreads, transaction costs, or market delay.
3. *Model Consistency:* The underlying volatility is constant or exogenous, independent of the dynamic positions of market participants.

In modern derivatives markets, these assumptions fail catastrophically:
- *Positive Feedback & Gamma Squeezes:* Option market makers and institutional hedgers hold massive concentrated gamma. When the underlying moves, mechanical delta-hedging trades push the asset *further in the direction of the trend*, driving extreme price surges (gamma squeezes) or sharp liquidity crashes (such as portfolio insurance during the 1987 crash or the March 2020 dislocations).
- *Hedging Frictions & Rebalancing Bands:* Proportional transaction costs render continuous replication impossible, requiring modified volatility regimes and asymptotic no-transaction bands.
- *Implied Volatility Dynamics:* Implied volatility curves shift as spot prices move (sticky strike vs. sticky delta), requiring the Delta Greek to be modified with a smile-skew adjustment (Minimum Variance Delta).

** Primary Objective of the Thesis
This Master's thesis investigates the theory, formulation, and empirical performance of **Modified Greeks** under realistic market conditions:
1. *Mathematical Formulation:* Derive and analyze nonlinear pricing PDEs (Frey-Stremme, Schönbucher-Wilmott, Leland, Whalley-Wilmott) that internalize market feedback, liquidity illiquidity, and transaction costs.
2. *Greeks Modification:* Systematically construct and contrast:
   - *Feedback-Adjusted Greeks* ($\Delta^*, \Gamma^*$) incorporating endogenous price impact.
   - *Friction-Adjusted Greeks* (Leland modified volatility and Whalley-Wilmott asymptotic hedging bands).
   - *Minimum-Variance Greeks* ($\Delta_{\text{MV}}$) accounting for local/implied volatility surface deformation.
3. *Agent-Based Market Microstructure Simulator:* Implement an event-driven order-book simulation populated by noise traders, fundamental investors, and dynamic option hedgers. Prove how modified Greeks mitigate hedging tracking error, prevent liquidity death spirals, and alter market stability.

* Mathematical Framework

** 1. Endogenous Asset Dynamics and the Nonlinear Black-Scholes PDE

Let $S_t$ denote the spot price of the underlying asset. In the presence of a large option market maker holding an aggregate delta-hedging position $\alpha \Delta(S_t, t)$ (where $\alpha > 0$ represents the scale of the hedged book), the price dynamics incorporate an endogenous market impact term:
$$ dS_t = \mu S_t \, dt + \sigma S_t \, dW_t + \lambda(S_t) \, d\big(\alpha \Delta(S_t, t)\big), $$
where:
- $\mu$ is the drift, $\sigma > 0$ is the unperturbed reference volatility,
- $W_t$ is a standard Brownian motion under $\mathbb{P}$,
- $\lambda(S_t)$ is the market illiquidity parameter (price impact per share traded).

Applying Itô's Lemma to the hedge ratio $\Delta(S_t, t) = \frac{\partial V}{\partial S}(S_t, t)$:
$$ d\Delta(S_t, t) = \frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial t} \, dt + \frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial S} \, dS_t + \frac{1}{2} \frac{\partial^2 \Delta}{\partial S^2} \, d\langle S \rangle_t = \frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial t} \, dt + \Gamma(S_t, t) \, dS_t + \frac{1}{2} \frac{\partial \Gamma}{\partial S} \, d\langle S \rangle_t. $$

Collecting the $dS_t$ terms on the left-hand side:
$$ \Big(1 - \alpha \lambda(S_t) \Gamma(S_t, t)\Big) \, dS_t = \left(\mu S_t + \alpha \lambda(S_t) \frac{\partial \Delta}{\partial t}\right) dt + \sigma S_t \, dW_t + \mathcal{O}(dt). $$

Defining the dimensionless *feedback factor* $\rho(S_t) = \frac{\alpha \lambda(S_t)}{S_t}$, the effective continuous diffusion of the stock becomes:
$$ dS_t = \tilde{\mu}_t S_t \, dt + \sigma_{\text{eff}}\big(S_t, t, \Gamma\big) S_t \, dW_t, $$
where the **effective volatility** is nonlinearly coupled to the option's Gamma:
$$ \sigma_{\text{eff}}(S, t, \Gamma) = \frac{\sigma}{1 - \rho(S) S \Gamma(S, t)}. $$

*** The Nonlinear Pricing PDE
Under risk-neutral pricing arguments (Frey & Stremme, Schönbucher & Wilmott), the option value function $V(S, t)$ satisfies:
$$ \frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{\sigma}{1 - \rho(S) S \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2}} \right)^2 S^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} + r S \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} - r V = 0, $$
subject to terminal payoff condition $V(S, T) = \Phi(S)$.

*Critical Mathematical Phenomenon:*
When $1 - \rho(S) S \Gamma \to 0$, the effective volatility explodes ($\sigma_{\text{eff}} \to \infty$). This represents a **liquidity breakdown** or **gamma singularity**, where the market lacks sufficient depth to absorb the hedger's mandatory rebalancing orders.

** 2. The Family of Modified Greeks

Hedging using standard Black-Scholes sensitivities under endogenous feedback or friction creates systematic mishedging and severe PnL drag. The thesis focuses on deriving and implementing three distinct classes of modified Greeks:

*** A. Feedback-Adjusted Greeks
Accounting for the fact that buying/selling shares moves the underlying price:
1. *Feedback-Adjusted Delta ($\Delta^*$):*
   $$ \Delta^*(S, t) = \frac{\Delta_{\text{BS}}(S, t)}{1 - \rho(S) S \, \Gamma_{\text{BS}}(S, t)}. $$
   - If the hedger is *short gamma* ($\Gamma > 0$), $\Delta^* > \Delta_{\text{BS}}$: the hedger must trade *more aggressively* to compensate for their own adverse price impact.
   - If the hedger is *long gamma* ($\Gamma < 0$), $\Delta^* < \Delta_{\text{BS}}$: price impact works in the hedger's favor, dampening required order volume.
2. *Feedback-Adjusted Gamma ($\Gamma^*$):*
   $$ \Gamma^*(S, t) = \frac{\Gamma_{\text{BS}}(S, t)}{\left(1 - \rho(S) S \, \Gamma_{\text{BS}}(S, t)\right)^3}. $$
3. *Liquidity Vega ($\mathcal{V}^*$):*
   Measures sensitivity of the portfolio not only to fundamental volatility $\sigma$, but to shifts in market depth $\lambda$.

*** B. Friction-Adjusted Greeks & No-Transaction Bands (Leland / Whalley-Wilmott)
Under proportional transaction costs $c > 0$ per share traded and discrete rebalancing interval $\delta t$:
1. *Leland Modified Volatility:*
   $$ \hat{\sigma}^2 = \sigma^2 \left( 1 + \sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}} \frac{c}{\sigma \sqrt{\delta t}} \operatorname{sgn}(\Gamma) \right). $$
2. *Whalley-Wilmott Asymptotic Hedging Band:*
   Instead of continuously trading to exact $\Delta$, the hedger only trades when their current stock holding $\Delta_{\text{actual}}$ drifts outside an optimal band $[\Delta - h, \Delta + h]$:
   $$ h(S, t) = \left( \frac{3 \, e^{-r(T-t)} \, c \, S \, \Gamma^2}{2 \, \gamma} \right)^{1/3}, $$
   where $\gamma > 0$ is the agent's absolute risk aversion parameter.

*** C. Minimum-Variance Delta ($\Delta_{\text{MV}}$) under Smile Dynamics
When market volatility smiles move with spot (e.g. sticky-strike vs. sticky-delta):
$$ \Delta_{\text{MV}} = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(dV, dS)}{\operatorname{Var}(dS)} = \Delta_{\text{BS}} + \mathcal{V}_{\text{BS}} \frac{\partial \sigma_{\text{impl}}}{\partial S}. $$
This modification captures the correlation between underlying returns and volatility shifts, minimizing hedging variance.

** Simulation Engine Specifications
1. *Time Discretization:* High-frequency sub-steps $\Delta t$ (e.g., tick level or 1-second intervals) combined with option rebalancing intervals $\delta t \ge \Delta t$.
2. *Agent Behaviors:*
   - *Noise / Retail Traders:* Generate stochastic order flow modeled as an inhomogeneous Poisson or Hawkes process, purchasing out-of-the-money calls or executing liquidity shocks.
   - *Fundamental Traders:* Provide resting liquidity around an unobserved fundamental asset value $V_t$, dampening extreme price deviations.
   - *Option Market Maker (OMM):* Maintains an inventory of short/long vanilla and barrier options across multiple strikes $K \in \{K_1, \dots, K_M\}$. Rebalances spot hedge according to:
     - Strategy 0: Naive unhedged.
     - Strategy 1: Classical Black-Scholes Delta ($\Delta_{\text{BS}}$).
     - Strategy 2: Feedback-Adjusted Delta ($\Delta^*$).
     - Strategy 3: Whalley-Wilmott Band Hedging.
     - Strategy 4: Minimum-Variance Delta ($\Delta_{\text{MV}}$).

** Four High-Yield Stress Experiments
1. *The Gamma Squeeze Experiment:* Inject a surge of retail call purchases on near-the-money options. Measure how standard $\Delta_{\text{BS}}$ causes explosive price cascades vs how $\Delta^*$ moderates order execution or warns of liquidity collapse.
2. *The Flash Crash & Cascade:* Trigger an exogenous price decline. Track whether rapid delta liquidation by short-gamma hedgers causes endogenous crash amplification.
3. *Strike Pinning at Expiration:* Test whether delta rebalancing forces the underlying asset to converge toward the strike $K$ as $t \to T$, quantifying pinning probability as a function of market depth $\lambda$.
4. *Hedging PnL & Cost-Variance Frontier:* For $10^5$ Monte Carlo simulated market histories, plot the empirical distribution of hedger PnL:
   $$ \Pi_T = V_0 - \Phi(S_T) + \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \Delta_{t_k} (S_{t_{k+1}} - S_{t_k}) - \text{Transaction Costs} - \text{Slippage}. $$
   Benchmark standard Greeks vs modified Greeks across mean PnL, Value-at-Risk (VaR), and tracking error variance.
