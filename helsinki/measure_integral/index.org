* Welcome & Strategic Gap Analysis

** Can You Self-Study This Prerequisite Concurrently?
*Yes, absolutely.* Here is the realistic, pedagogical reality:

1. *The Two Courses are Synergistic, Not Disconnected:*
   The advanced course /Measure, probability, and real analysis/ (5 cr) starts directly from *abstract measure spaces* $(X, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$, \(\sigma\)-algebras, and probability measures. It does /not/ assume you have memorized specialized Euclidean geometry tricks; rather, it assumes you have the *conceptual intuition* of what a measure is, how null sets behave, why we integrate using simple functions, and how to pass limits under the integral sign using the Big Three (MCT, Fatou, DCT).
2. *Concrete Intuition Gives You an Unfair Advantage:*
   Students who jump straight into abstract $(X, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$ often struggle because they lack concrete mental pictures. By studying /Measure and Integral/ (`MeasInt17.pdf`) alongside the advanced course, $\mathbb{R}^n$ with Lebesgue measure $m$ serves as your concrete testing ground. Every time the professor introduces an abstract theorem, you will already understand why it was invented in $\mathbb{R}^n$.
3. *Identical Integration Engine:*
   The proofs of the Monotone Convergence Theorem (MCT), Fatou's Lemma, Dominated Convergence Theorem (DCT), and Fubini-Tonelli theorems in the abstract course are *word-for-word identical* to the Euclidean proofs presented in these notes!

* The Core Dictionary: Measure Theory $\longleftrightarrow$ Probability Theory

Keep this translation matrix in mind throughout your concurrent study:

| Real Analysis / Measure Theory | Symbol | Probability Theory | Symbol |
|--------------------------------+--------+--------------------+--------|
| Base space | $X$ | Sample space | $\Omega$ |
| Measurable set | $E \in \Sigma$ | Event | $A \in \mathcal{F}$ |
| \(\sigma\)-algebra | $\Sigma$ | Information field / \(\sigma\)-algebra | $\mathcal{F}$ |
| Measure with $\mu(X) = 1$ | $\mu$ | Probability measure | $\mathbb{P}$ |
| Measurable function | $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$ | Random variable | $X : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ |
| Non-negative simple function | $\sum a_i \chi_{A_i}$ | Discrete random variable | $\sum a_i \mathbf{1}_{A_i}$ |
| Lebesgue integral $\int f \, d\mu$ | $\int_X f \, d\mu$ | Mathematical Expectation | $\mathbb{E}[X]$ |
| Almost everywhere ($a.e.$) | a.e. | Almost surely ($a.s.$) | a.s. |
| Convergence in measure | $f_n \xrightarrow{\mu} f$ | Convergence in probability | $X_n \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}} X$ |
| \(L^1\)-norm convergence | $\lVert f_n - f \rVert_1 \to 0$ | Convergence in mean | $\mathbb{E}[\lVert X_n - X \rVert] \to 0$ |
| Product measure | $\mu \otimes \nu$ | Joint distribution of independent variables | $\mathbb{P}_X \otimes \mathbb{P}_Y$ |
