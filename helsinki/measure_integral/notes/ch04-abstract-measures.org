* Overview

** What problem does this chapter solve?
So far, we have focused primarily on the Lebesgue measure $m$ on Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n$. 
However, modern mathematics and probability require measures on diverse spaces:
- In probability theory, we measure abstract sample spaces $\Omega$ with total probability $P(\Omega) = 1$.
- In discrete mathematics, we use counting measure.
- In physics, we measure phase spaces, manifolds, or function spaces.

Rather than reproving foundational theorems for each setting, we isolate the essential axioms that make measure theory work. 
This yields the concept of an *abstract measure space* $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$.

The crowning achievement of this chapter is the study of *limits of measures*:
- If a sequence of sets expands ($E_1 \subset E_2 \subset \dots$), does the measure of the union equal the limit of the measures?
- If a sequence of sets shrinks ($E_1 \supset E_2 \supset \dots$), does the measure of the intersection equal the limit of the measures?

We will discover that continuity from below *always* holds, while continuity from above holds *if and only if* at least one of the sets in the chain has finite measure!

* Abstract Measure Spaces

** Formal Definition
#+begin_quote
*Definition 4.1 (Measure Space).* Let $X$ be a non-empty set and let $\mathcal{M}$ be a $\sigma$-algebra of subsets of $X$. 
A function $\mu: \mathcal{M} \to [0, +\infty]$ is called a *measure* on $(X, \mathcal{M})$ if:
1. *Null Empty Set:* $\mu(\emptyset) = 0$.
2. *Countable Additivity ($\sigma$-additivity):* For every sequence $E_1, E_2, E_3, \dots \in \mathcal{M}$ of pairwise disjoint sets:
   \[
   \mu\left( \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty E_j \right) = \sum_{j=1}^\infty \mu(E_j).
   \]
The triple $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ is called a *measure space*. The pair $(X, \mathcal{M})$ is called a *measurable space*, and elements of $\mathcal{M}$ are called *measurable sets*.
#+end_quote

** Classification of Measures
- *Finite Measure:* $\mu$ is a finite measure if $\mu(X) < \infty$.
- *Probability Measure:* $\mu$ is a probability measure (often denoted $P$) if $\mu(X) = 1$.
- *$\sigma$-Finite Measure:* $\mu$ is $\sigma$-finite if $X$ can be written as a countable union of sets of finite measure:
  \[
  X = \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty X_k, \quad \text{with } X_k \in \mathcal{M} \text{ and } \mu(X_k) < \infty \text{ for all } k \in \mathbb{N}.
  \]
- *Complete Measure Space:* $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ is complete if every subset of a null set is measurable:
  \[
  E \in \mathcal{M}, \, \mu(E) = 0, \, A \subset E \implies A \in \mathcal{M} \text{ (and hence } \mu(A) = 0\text{)}.
  \]

* Canonical Examples

** Example 4.2 (Lebesgue Measure Space)
$(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^n), m)$, where $\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is the $\sigma$-algebra of Lebesgue measurable sets and $m$ is Lebesgue measure.
- $m(\mathbb{R}^n) = +\infty$, so it is not a finite measure.
- However, $\mathbb{R}^n = \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty [-k, k]^n$, where $m([-k, k]^n) = (2k)^n < \infty$. Thus Lebesgue measure is *$\sigma$-finite*.
- By Theorem 2.2, Lebesgue measure is *complete*.

** Example 4.3 (Counting Measure)
Let $X$ be any non-empty set and $\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{P}(X)$. For any $A \subset X$, define:
\[
\mu_{\text{count}}(A) = \begin{cases} |A|, & \text{if } A \text{ is finite}, \\ +\infty, & \text{if } A \text{ is infinite}. \end{cases}
\]
- If $X$ is countable, $\mu_{\text{count}}$ is $\sigma$-finite.
- If $X$ is uncountable (e.g. $X = \mathbb{R}$), $\mu_{\text{count}}$ is *not* $\sigma$-finite!

** Example 4.4 (Dirac Measure / Point Mass)
Fix a point $x_0 \in X$ and let $\mathcal{M}$ be any $\sigma$-algebra on $X$. Define:
\[
\delta_{x_0}(A) = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{if } x_0 \in A, \\ 0, & \text{if } x_0 \notin A. \end{cases}
\]
This is a probability measure, concentrated entirely at the single point $x_0$.

* Elementary Properties of Measures

#+begin_quote
*Proposition 4.5.* Let $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ be a measure space, and let $A, B \in \mathcal{M}$.
1. *Monotonicity:* If $A \subset B$, then $\mu(A) \le \mu(B)$.
2. *Subtractivity:* If $A \subset B$ and $\mu(A) < \infty$, then
   \[
   \mu(B \setminus A) = \mu(B) - \mu(A).
   \]
3. *Finite Subadditivity:* For any $A_1, \dots, A_k \in \mathcal{M}$:
   \[
   \mu\left( \bigcup_{i=1}^k A_i \right) \le \sum_{i=1}^k \mu(A_i).
   \]
4. *Countable Subadditivity:* For any sequence $A_1, A_2, A_3, \dots \in \mathcal{M}$:
   \[
   \mu\left( \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty A_j \right) \le \sum_{j=1}^\infty \mu(A_j).
   \]
#+end_quote

#+begin_proof
1. Decompose $B = A \cup (B \setminus A)$. This is a disjoint union of measurable sets. By additivity:
   \[
   \mu(B) = \mu(A) + \mu(B \setminus A).
   \]
   Since $\mu(B \setminus A) \ge 0$, we have $\mu(B) \ge \mu(A)$.
2. If $\mu(A) < \infty$, we can subtract $\mu(A)$ from both sides of $\mu(B) = \mu(A) + \mu(B \setminus A)$, giving $\mu(B \setminus A) = \mu(B) - \mu(A)$.
3. and 4. follow by disjointifying the sets: let $F_1 = A_1$ and $F_j = A_j \setminus (\bigcup_{i=1}^{j-1} A_i)$. Then the $F_j$ are disjoint, $F_j \subset A_j$, and $\bigcup F_j = \bigcup A_j$. By additivity and monotonicity:
   \[
   \mu\left( \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty A_j \right) = \mu\left( \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty F_j \right) = \sum_{j=1}^\infty \mu(F_j) \le \sum_{j=1}^\infty \mu(A_j).
   \]
#+end_proof

* Continuity of Measures

** Continuity from Below (Increasing Sequences of Sets)

#+begin_quote
*Theorem 4.6 (Continuity from Below).* Let $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ be a measure space. 
Suppose $E_1 \subset E_2 \subset E_3 \subset \dots$ is an increasing sequence of measurable sets ($E_j \nearrow E$), and let $E = \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty E_j$.
Then:
\[
\lim_{j \to \infty} \mu(E_j) = \mu(E) = \mu\left( \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty E_j \right).
\]
#+end_quote

#+begin_proof
1. Disjointify the sequence: set $F_1 = E_1$ and $F_j = E_j \setminus E_{j-1}$ for $j \ge 2$.
2. The sets $F_j$ are pairwise disjoint and belong to $\mathcal{M}$.
3. For each $k \in \mathbb{N}$, the union is telescoping:
   \[
   \bigcup_{j=1}^k F_j = E_k.
   \]
   By finite additivity:
   \[
   \mu(E_k) = \sum_{j=1}^k \mu(F_j).
   \]
4. Moreover, $\bigcup_{j=1}^\infty F_j = \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty E_j = E$.
5. By countable additivity of $\mu$:
   \[
   \mu(E) = \sum_{j=1}^\infty \mu(F_j) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \sum_{j=1}^k \mu(F_j) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \mu(E_k).
   \]
This completes the proof.
#+end_proof

** Continuity from Above (Decreasing Sequences of Sets)

#+begin_quote
*Theorem 4.7 (Continuity from Above).* Let $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ be a measure space.
Suppose $E_1 \supset E_2 \supset E_3 \supset \dots$ is a decreasing sequence of measurable sets ($E_j \searrow E$), and let $E = \bigcap_{j=1}^\infty E_j$.
*Assume that $\mu(E_1) < \infty$* (or $\mu(E_k) < \infty$ for some $k$).
Then:
\[
\lim_{j \to \infty} \mu(E_j) = \mu(E) = \mu\left( \bigcap_{j=1}^\infty E_j \right).
\]
#+end_quote

#+begin_proof
Without loss of generality, assume $\mu(E_1) < \infty$.
1. Convert the decreasing sequence into an increasing sequence by taking complements relative to $E_1$:
   Define $A_j = E_1 \setminus E_j$ for each $j \in \mathbb{N}$.
2. Since $E_1 \supset E_2 \supset E_3 \dots$, the complements increase:
   \[
   A_1 \subset A_2 \subset A_3 \subset \dots
   \]
3. By de Morgan's laws:
   \[
   \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty A_j = \bigcup_{j=1}^\infty (E_1 \setminus E_j) = E_1 \setminus \left( \bigcap_{j=1}^\infty E_j \right) = E_1 \setminus E.
   \]
4. Apply Theorem 4.6 (Continuity from Below) to the sequence $\{A_j\}$:
   \[
   \lim_{j \to \infty} \mu(A_j) = \mu(E_1 \setminus E).
   \]
5. Since $\mu(E_1) < \infty$, by subtractivity (Proposition 4.5):
   \[
   \mu(A_j) = \mu(E_1 \setminus E_j) = \mu(E_1) - \mu(E_j),
   \]
   and
   \[
   \mu(E_1 \setminus E) = \mu(E_1) - \mu(E).
   \]
6. Substituting these into the limit equation:
   \[
   \lim_{j \to \infty} \bigl( \mu(E_1) - \mu(E_j) \bigr) = \mu(E_1) - \mu(E).
   \]
7. Since $\mu(E_1) < \infty$, we can cancel $\mu(E_1)$ from both sides:
   \[
   -\lim_{j \to \infty} \mu(E_j) = -\mu(E) \implies \lim_{j \to \infty} \mu(E_j) = \mu(E).
   \]
#+end_proof

* The Infinite Counterexample

> [!CAUTION]
> The condition $\mu(E_1) < \infty$ in Theorem 4.7 is **strictly necessary**! 
> If all sets have infinite measure, continuity from above can fail spectacularly.

*Counterexample (Fall 2026 Lectures, p. 39--40):*
Consider the real line $(\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{M}, m)$ with Lebesgue measure.
For each $j \in \mathbb{N}$, define the half-line:
\[
E_j = [j, +\infty[.
\]
Notice:
1. The sequence is decreasing:
   \[
   [1, \infty[ \;\supset\; [2, \infty[ \;\supset\; [3, \infty[ \;\supset\; \dots
   \]
2. What is the intersection?
   For any $x \in \mathbb{R}$, by the Archimedean property there exists an integer $j > x$, which means $x \notin E_j$. 
   Therefore, no real number belongs to all $E_j$:
   \[
   E = \bigcap_{j=1}^\infty E_j = \emptyset.
   \]
   Thus the measure of the intersection is:
   \[
   m(E) = m(\emptyset) = 0.
   \]
3. However, what is the measure of each individual set $E_j$?
   Each $E_j$ is an infinite ray, so:
   \[
   m(E_j) = +\infty \quad \text{for every } j \in \mathbb{N}.
   \]
4. Taking the limit:
   \[
   \lim_{j \to \infty} m(E_j) = \lim_{j \to \infty} (+\infty) = +\infty \neq 0 = m\left( \bigcap_{j=1}^\infty E_j \right).
   \]
The limit of the measures is $+\infty$, but the measure of the limit set is $0$!
This proves that without $\mu(E_1) < \infty$, continuity from above fails completely.

* Worked Exercises

** Exercise 4.1 (Homework 4, Problem 2: Squeezing Sets by Sequences)
Assume that $E_1, E_2, E_3, \dots \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ are Lebesgue measurable sets and that
\[
\sum_{j=1}^\infty m(E_j) < \infty.
\]
Define the set of points that belong to infinitely many $E_j$:
\[
E_\infty = \limsup_{j \to \infty} E_j = \bigcap_{k=1}^\infty \left( \bigcup_{j=k}^\infty E_j \right).
\]
Prove that $m(E_\infty) = 0$. (This is the set-theoretic *Borel--Cantelli Lemma*!)

*Solution:*
1. For each $k \in \mathbb{N}$, define $A_k = \bigcup_{j=k}^\infty E_j$.
2. The sequence $\{A_k\}_{k=1}^\infty$ is decreasing:
   \[
   A_1 \supset A_2 \supset A_3 \supset \dots
   \]
   and by definition $E_\infty = \bigcap_{k=1}^\infty A_k$.
3. By countable subadditivity:
   \[
   m(A_k) = m\left( \bigcup_{j=k}^\infty E_j \right) \le \sum_{j=k}^\infty m(E_j).
   \]
4. Since the series $\sum_{j=1}^\infty m(E_j) < \infty$ converges, the tail of the series converges to zero:
   \[
   \lim_{k \to \infty} \sum_{j=k}^\infty m(E_j) = 0.
   \]
5. In particular, for $k = 1$, $m(A_1) \le \sum_{j=1}^\infty m(E_j) < \infty$.
6. Since $\{A_k\}$ is a decreasing sequence of measurable sets with $m(A_1) < \infty$, Theorem 4.7 (Continuity from Above) applies:
   \[
   m(E_\infty) = m\left( \bigcap_{k=1}^\infty A_k \right) = \lim_{k \to \infty} m(A_k) \le \lim_{k \to \infty} \sum_{j=k}^\infty m(E_j) = 0.
   \]
Since measure is non-negative, $m(E_\infty) = 0$.

** Exercise 4.2 (Liminf of Sets)
Let $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ be a measure space. For any sequence of sets $\{E_j\}$, define
\[
\liminf_{j \to \infty} E_j = \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty \left( \bigcap_{j=k}^\infty E_j \right).
\]
Prove that $\mu\left( \liminf_{j \to \infty} E_j \right) \le \liminf_{j \to \infty} \mu(E_j)$. (Fatou's lemma for sets!)

*Solution:*
1. Let $B_k = \bigcap_{j=k}^\infty E_j$.
2. The sequence $\{B_k\}_{k=1}^\infty$ is increasing: $B_1 \subset B_2 \subset B_3 \subset \dots$.
3. By Theorem 4.6 (Continuity from Below):
   \[
   \mu\left( \liminf_{j \to \infty} E_j \right) = \mu\left( \bigcup_{k=1}^\infty B_k \right) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \mu(B_k).
   \]
4. For every $j \ge k$, $B_k \subset E_j$. By monotonicity:
   \[
   \mu(B_k) \le \mu(E_j) \quad \text{for all } j \ge k.
   \]
5. Taking the infimum over all $j \ge k$:
   \[
   \mu(B_k) \le \inf_{j \ge k} \mu(E_j).
   \]
6. Taking the limit as $k \to \infty$:
   \[
   \mu\left( \liminf_{j \to \infty} E_j \right) = \lim_{k \to \infty} \mu(B_k) \le \lim_{k \to \infty} \inf_{j \ge k} \mu(E_j) = \liminf_{j \to \infty} \mu(E_j).
   \]

* Summary Cheat-Sheet

#+caption: Measure Space Axioms and Limits Reference
#+ATTR_LATEX: :environment tabularx :width \textwidth :align l >{\RaggedRight}X :booktabs t
| Property | Formula | Requirement / Warning |
|----------+---------+-----------------------|
| *Measure Space* | $(X, \mathcal{M}, \mu)$ | $\mu \ge 0$, $\mu(\emptyset) = 0$, countably additive on $\mathcal{M}$. |
| *Monotonicity* | $A \subset B \implies \mu(A) \le \mu(B)$ | Always holds. |
| *Subtractivity* | $\mu(B \setminus A) = \mu(B) - \mu(A)$ | Requires $A \subset B$ and $\mu(A) < \infty$. |
| *Continuity from Below* | $E_j \nearrow E \implies \mu(E_j) \to \mu(E)$ | *Always holds*, even if $\mu(E) = \infty$. |
| *Continuity from Above* | $E_j \searrow E \implies \mu(E_j) \to \mu(E)$ | *Requires $\mu(E_1) < \infty$!* |
| *Counterexample* | $E_j = [j, \infty[$ in $\mathbb{R}$ | $\bigcap E_j = \emptyset$ (measure $0$), but $\lim m(E_j) = \infty$. |
| *Borel--Cantelli* | $\sum \mu(E_j) < \infty \implies \mu(\limsup E_j) = 0$ | Points belonging to infinitely many sets form a null set. |
| *Completeness* | $N \subset E$ with $\mu(E) = 0 \implies N \in \mathcal{M}$ | Lebesgue measure is complete; Borel measure is not. |
