* How to Use These Notes

These notes provide a completely self-contained, rigorous, yet intuitive exposition of the course *Measure and Integral* (/Mitta ja integraali/, University of Helsinki, Fall 2026), based directly on:
- The Fall 2026 course lecture slides: =Lectures.pdf= (Part I: chapters 1--2) and =-Lectures Part II.pdf= (Part II: chapters 3--4),
- Ilkka Holopainen's lecture notes (=MeasInt17.pdf=),
- The Fall 2026 homework problem sets (HW1--HW6) and exam (=Measure_Integral_2K26.pdf=).

Every chapter is written with the following pedagogy:
1. *Intuition & Motivation first:* Before presenting technical definitions, we explain *why* the concept was invented, what failure of naive intuition it prevents, and the geometric or probabilistic mental picture.
2. *Rigor without skips:* All definitions and theorems are stated with exact mathematical hypotheses. Proofs are presented step by step without dismissing crucial details as "trivial".
3. *Examples and Non-Examples:* Every concept is illustrated with concrete calculations and counterexamples showing why each hypothesis is strictly necessary.
4. *Worked Exercises with Full Solutions:* Each chapter concludes with carefully chosen problems reflecting homework assignments and past exam questions, accompanied by step-by-step solutions.
5. *Summary Cheat-Sheets:* A reference table at the end of each chapter gives a quick review of definitions, theorems, and conditions.

*Suggested Study Paths:*
- *Foundations of Measure (Chapters 0--4):* Sets and countability $\to$ Outer measure on $\mathbb{R}^n$ $\to$ Carathéodory's criterion and measurable sets $\to$ Borel sets and regularity $\to$ Abstract measures and continuity of measures.
- *Integration & Limit Theorems (Chapters 5--8):* Measurable functions $\to$ Simple functions and approximation $\to$ Lebesgue integral for non-negative functions (MCT, Fatou) $\to$ General Lebesgue integral (DCT, parameter integrals).
- *Advanced Topics (Chapters 9--10):* Fubini--Tonelli product integration $\to$ $L^p$ spaces, inequalities, and Riesz--Fischer completeness.

* Conceptual Architecture of Measure Theory

The entire theory developed in these notes solves a single overarching historical problem: *How do we assign a consistent size ("volume" or "integral") to arbitrary geometric sets and functions without running into logical contradictions?*

#+begin_src text
  Elementary Volumes on n-intervals \ell(I)
                  │
                  ▼
  Lebesgue Outer Measure m*(A) = inf sum \ell(I_j)   (defined for ALL subsets A of R^n)
                  │
                  │ [Defect: m* is only countably SUB-additive, not additive on all sets!]
                  ▼
  Carathéodory Splitting Criterion: m*(A) = m*(A ∩ E) + m*(A \ E)
                  │
                  ▼
  Lebesgue Measurable Sets M(R^n)   (a σ-algebra! m is strictly countably additive on M)
                  │
                  ├── Contains all Borel sets B(R^n) (open, closed, compact, G_δ, F_σ)
                  └── Excludes pathological sets (Vitali non-measurable set)
                  │
                  ▼
  Measurable Functions f: A -> [-∞, ∞]   (preimages of open sets are measurable)
                  │
                  ▼
  Approximation by Simple Functions: s_k \nearrow f  (s_k = sum a_i \chi_{A_i})
                  │
                  ▼
  Lebesgue Integral:
    1. Simple functions:  \int s = sum a_i m(A_i)
    2. Non-negative:      \int f = sup {\int s : 0 \le s \le f, s simple}
    3. General:           \int f = \int f^+ - \int f^-   (\int |f| < \infty)
                  │
                  ▼
  Triumphs over Riemann Integration:
    - Monotone Convergence Theorem (MCT)
    - Fatou's Lemma
    - Dominated Convergence Theorem (DCT)
    - Complete Function Spaces: L^p(E) is a Banach space!
    - Product Integration: Tonelli and Fubini Theorems
#+end_src
