* Course Information and Overview

- *Course Title:* Measure, Probability, and Real Analysis (Mitta, todennäköisyys ja reaalianalyysi)
- *Course Code:* MAST31001 / MAST31002 (5 cr)
- *Institution:* Department of Mathematics and Statistics, University of Helsinki
- *Formal Prerequisites:* MAT21007 / BSMA2003 / MAST30101 Measure and Integral (Mitta ja integraali)
- *Official Syllabus & Planned Contents:*
  1. Measure theory beyond the Lebesgue case: general measure spaces $(X, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$ and \(\sigma\)-algebras
  2. Probabilities, expectations, cumulative distribution functions (CDFs), and Lebesgue-Stieltjes integrals
  3. Monotone class arguments (Dynkin's \(\pi\)-\(\lambda\) lemma) and uniqueness of measures
  4. Abstract integration and passage to the limit under the integral sign (abstract MCT, Fatou, DCT)
  5. Carathéodory's extension theorem, construction of finite and infinite product measures, Fubini's theorem
  6. Independence of random variables and product spaces
  7. $L^p$ spaces and their completeness (Riesz-Fischer); convergence of random variables (almost surely, in probability, in $L^p$, in distribution)
  8. Regularity of Borel measures and approximation of measurable functions by smooth functions
  9. Borel-Cantelli lemmas and the Laws of Large Numbers (WLLN & SLLN)

* Realistic Assessment: Can You Follow This Course With Your Current Background?

*Verdict:* *Yes, definitely.*

Here is why:
1. *Synergistic, Not Disconnected:*
   The advanced course does *not* assume you remember specialized Euclidean geometric tricks. Instead, it re-develops measure theory at the **abstract level**:
   $$ (X, \mathcal{F}, \mu) \quad \text{instead of} \quad (\mathbb{R}^n, \mathrm{Leb}(\mathbb{R}^n), m). $$
2. *Concrete Intuition Gives You an Edge:*
   Students who jump straight into abstract measure spaces without concrete Euclidean grounding often get lost in symbol manipulation. Having the concrete models from [[file:../measure_integral/index.org][Measure and Integral]]—where measures are lengths, areas, and volumes—gives you the geometric mental picture for every abstract theorem.
3. *Identical Integration Machinery:*
   The proofs of the Monotone Convergence Theorem (MCT), Fatou's Lemma, and the Dominated Convergence Theorem (DCT) in an abstract space $(X, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$ are **word-for-word identical** to those in [[file:../measure_integral/03_lebesgue_integration_and_convergence_theorems.org][Module 3]].

* Strategic Checklist Before Week 1

Ensure you have these four foundational concepts solid before stepping into your first lecture:

** 1. The \(\sigma\)-Algebra Definition & Generation
- [ ] *The 3 Axioms:* Know the definition of a \(\sigma\)-algebra $\mathcal{F}$ on $\Omega$ cold:
  1. $\Omega \in \mathcal{F}$,
  2. $A \in \mathcal{F} \implies A^c \in \mathcal{F}$ (closed under complements),
  3. $A_1, A_2, \dots \in \mathcal{F} \implies \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n \in \mathcal{F}$ (closed under **countable** unions).
- [ ] *Generated \(\sigma\)-algebra $\sigma(\mathcal{E})$:* Understand that $\sigma(\mathcal{E})$ is the *smallest* \(\sigma\)-algebra containing a family of sets $\mathcal{E}$ (the intersection of all \(\sigma\)-algebras containing $\mathcal{E}$).
- [ ] *The Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$:* Know that it is generated by open intervals $]a, b[$, but equivalently generated by closed rays $]-\infty, t]$.

** 2. Preimages: The Engine of Measurable Functions (Random Variables)
- [ ] *Why preimages preserve all set operations:*
  $$ f^{-1}(B^c) = (f^{-1}(B))^c, \qquad f^{-1}\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty B_n\right) = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty f^{-1}(B_n), \qquad f^{-1}\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty B_n\right) = \bigcap_{n=1}^\infty f^{-1}(B_n). $$
- [ ] *Random Variable as a Measurable Function:*
  A mapping $X : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ is a random variable $\iff X^{-1}(B) \in \mathcal{F}$ for all $B \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$.
- [ ] *The Sublevel Shortcut:* Because rays $]-\infty, t]$ generate $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})$, to verify that $X$ is a random variable, you only ever need to check:
  $$ \{\omega \in \Omega : X(\omega) \le t\} \in \mathcal{F} \quad \text{for all } t \in \mathbb{R}. $$
  *(This is why the Cumulative Distribution Function $F(t) = \mathbb{P}(X \le t)$ is well-defined!)*

** 3. Continuity of Measure (Limits of Sets)
- [ ] *Continuity from below:* If $A_1 \subseteq A_2 \subseteq A_3 \subseteq \dots$, then $\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^\infty A_n\right) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \mathbb{P}(A_n)$.
- [ ] *Continuity from above:* If $A_1 \supseteq A_2 \supseteq A_3 \supseteq \dots$, then $\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^\infty A_n\right) = \lim_{n\to\infty} \mathbb{P}(A_n)$.
  *(Essential for tail events and the Borel-Cantelli lemma).*

** 4. Sets of Measure Zero & "Almost Surely" ($a.s.$)
- [ ] Understand that if an event $N$ has $\mathbb{P}(N) = 0$, what happens inside $N$ can be completely ignored.
- [ ] $X = Y \text{ a.s.} \iff \mathbb{P}(\{\omega : X(\omega) \ne Y(\omega)\}) = 0$.

* What You Can Safely Postpone

You do **not** need to study these before Week 1; the course will develop them systematically:
1. *Carathéodory's Extension Theorem proof:* The course will state or prove this in Weeks 2–3 when constructing product measures and Lebesgue-Stieltjes measures. Just understand the *result* (a pre-measure extends uniquely to a \(\sigma\)-algebra).
2. *Dynkin's \(\pi\)-\(\lambda\) lemma proof:* This will be taught explicitly as the standard tool to prove uniqueness of measures on $\sigma(\mathcal{P})$.
3. *Infinite Kolmogorov extension:* This comes in later weeks when defining infinite sequences of independent random variables $(X_1, X_2, \dots)$.

* Master Dictionary: Measure Theory $\longleftrightarrow$ Probability Theory

#+caption: Complete Dictionary between Real Analysis and Probability Theory
| Real Analysis / Measure Theory | Symbol | Probability Theory | Symbol |
|--------------------------------+--------+--------------------+--------|
| Base space | $X$ | Sample space | $\Omega$ |
| Measurable set | $E \in \Sigma$ | Event | $A \in \mathcal{F}$ |
| \(\sigma\)-algebra | $\Sigma$ | Information field / \(\sigma\)-algebra | $\mathcal{F}$ |
| Measure with $\mu(X) = 1$ | $\mu$ | Probability measure | $\mathbb{P}$ |
| Measurable function | $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$ | Random variable | $X : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ |
| Non-negative simple function | $\sum a_i \chi_{A_i}$ | Discrete random variable | $\sum a_i \mathbf{1}_{A_i}$ |
| Lebesgue integral $\int f \, d\mu$ | $\int_X f \, d\mu$ | Mathematical Expectation | $\mathbb{E}[X]$ |
| Almost everywhere ($a.e.$) | a.e. | Almost surely ($a.s.$) | a.s. |
| Convergence in measure | $f_n \xrightarrow{\mu} f$ | Convergence in probability | $X_n \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}} X$ |
| \(L^1\)-norm convergence | $\lVert f_n - f \rVert_1 \to 0$ | Convergence in mean | $\mathbb{E}[\lVert X_n - X \rVert] \to 0$ |
| Product measure | $\mu \otimes \nu$ | Joint distribution of independent variables | $\mathbb{P}_X \otimes \mathbb{P}_Y$ |

* Primary Recommended Literature

1. *Schilling, R. L. (2017).* /Measures, Integrals and Martingales/ (2nd ed.). Cambridge University Press.
   - The clearest, most pedagogical modern textbook aligning abstract measure theory directly with probability and martingales. Contains full solutions to odd-numbered exercises!
2. *Ash, R. B., & Doléans-Dade, C. A. (2000).* /Probability and Measure Theory/ (2nd ed.). Academic Press.
   - Comprehensive reference used frequently in Finnish university graduate-level probability courses.
3. *Billingsley, P. (1995).* /Probability and Measure/ (3rd ed.). John Wiley & Sons.
   - The definitive classic for measure-theoretic probability, characteristic functions, and the central limit theorem.
4. *Folland, G. B. (1999).* /Real Analysis: Modern Techniques and Their Applications/ (2nd ed.). John Wiley & Sons.
   - The standard graduate-level reference for $L^p$ spaces, functional analysis, and product measures.
