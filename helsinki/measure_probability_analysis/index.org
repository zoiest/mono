* Core Syllabus Breakdown

** 1. Abstract Measure Spaces and \(\sigma\)-Algebras
- Definitions: measurable space $(X, \mathcal{F})$, measure space $(X, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$, finite, $\sigma$-finite, and probability measures ($\mathbb{P}(X) = 1$).
- Generated \(\sigma\)-algebras $\sigma(\mathcal{E})$ and the Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra $\mathcal{B}(X)$ on topological spaces.
- Continuity of measure: continuity from below ($\mu(A_n) \nearrow \mu(\bigcup A_n)$ for $A_n \nearrow A$) and continuity from above ($\mu(B_n) \searrow \mu(\bigcap B_n)$ for $B_n \searrow B$ with $\mu(B_1) < \infty$).

** 2. Random Variables, CDFs, and Lebesgue-Stieltjes Measures
- Measurable mappings $X: (\Omega, \mathcal{F}) \to (\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))$.
- Cumulative distribution function (CDF) $F(t) = \mathbb{P}(X \le t)$: right-continuity, monotonicity, limits at $\pm \infty$.
- The Lebesgue-Stieltjes measure $\mu_F$ associated with $F$, integrating expectations: $\mathbb{E}[g(X)] = \int_{\mathbb{R}} g(t) \, dF(t)$.

** 3. Monotone Class Arguments & Dynkin's \(\pi\)-\(\lambda\) Lemma
- Definition: $\pi$-systems (closed under finite intersections) and $\lambda$-systems (monotone classes closed under complements and disjoint countable unions).
- *Dynkin's \(\pi\)-\(\lambda\) Theorem:* If $\mathcal{P}$ is a $\pi$-system and $\mathcal{L}$ is a $\lambda$-system with $\mathcal{P} \subseteq \mathcal{L}$, then $\sigma(\mathcal{P}) \subseteq \mathcal{L}$.
- Uniqueness of measures: if two probability measures agree on a $\pi$-system that generates the \(\sigma\)-algebra, they are identical everywhere.

** 4. Abstract Integration and Passage to the Limit
- The Standard Machine: indicator functions $\mathbf{1}_E \longrightarrow$ simple functions $\sum a_i \mathbf{1}_{E_i} \longrightarrow$ non-negative functions $\sup \int \phi \longrightarrow$ integrable functions $f = f^+ - f^-$.
- Monotone Convergence Theorem (MCT) for sequences $0 \le f_n \nearrow f$.
- Fatou's Lemma: $\int \liminf f_n \le \liminf \int f_n$.
- Dominated Convergence Theorem (DCT): if $\lvert f_n \rvert \le g \in L^1$, then $\int f_n \to \int f$.

** 5. Carathéodory's Extension Theorem and Product Measures
- Pre-measures on algebras of sets.
- *Carathéodory Extension Theorem:* Any pre-measure on an algebra $\mathcal{A}$ extends uniquely to a measure on $\sigma(\mathcal{A})$.
- Product measure spaces $(X \times Y, \mathcal{F} \otimes \mathcal{G}, \mu \otimes \nu)$.
- Monotone class theorem for functions and Fubini-Tonelli theorems on general $\sigma$-finite measure spaces.

** 6. Independence of Random Variables and Product Spaces
- Independence of events, sub-\(\sigma\)-algebras $\mathcal{F}_1, \dots, \mathcal{F}_n$, and random variables.
- Factorization of joint distributions $\mathbb{P}_{(X, Y)} = \mathbb{P}_X \otimes \mathbb{P}_Y$ and expectations $\mathbb{E}[X Y] = \mathbb{E}[X] \mathbb{E}[Y]$.
- Infinite product spaces and the Kolmogorov Extension Theorem for stochastic processes $(X_t)_{t \in T}$.

** 7. \(L^p\) Spaces and Modes of Convergence
- Definition of \(L^p(X, \mu)\), Hölder's inequality, and Minkowski's inequality.
- Completeness: the Riesz-Fischer theorem (\(L^p\) is a Banach space for $1 \le p \le \infty$).
- Hierarchy of convergence:
  $$\text{Almost Sure (a.s.)} \quad \implies \quad \text{Convergence in Probability } (X_n \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}} X) \quad \implies \quad \text{Convergence in Distribution } (X_n \xrightarrow{d} X)$$
  $$L^p \text{ Convergence } (X_n \xrightarrow{L^p} X) \quad \implies \quad \text{Convergence in Probability } (X_n \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}} X)$$

** 8. Borel-Cantelli Lemmas and Laws of Large Numbers
- *First Borel-Cantelli Lemma:* $\sum \mathbb{P}(A_n) < \infty \implies \mathbb{P}(\limsup A_n) = 0$.
- *Second Borel-Cantelli Lemma:* For independent events, $\sum \mathbb{P}(A_n) = \infty \implies \mathbb{P}(\limsup A_n) = 1$.
- Markov and Chebyshev inequalities.
- Weak Law of Large Numbers (WLLN) via \(L^2\) bounds and characteristic functions.
- Strong Law of Large Numbers (SLLN) via truncation and Borel-Cantelli arguments.

* Master Dictionary: Measure Theory $\longleftrightarrow$ Probability Theory

| Real Analysis / Measure Theory | Symbol | Probability Theory | Symbol |
|--------------------------------+--------+--------------------+--------|
| Measure space | $(X, \mathcal{F}, \mu)$ | Probability space | $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})$ |
| Measurable set | $E \in \mathcal{F}$ | Event | $A \in \mathcal{F}$ |
| Measurable function | $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$ | Random variable | $X: \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ |
| Characteristic function $\mathbf{1}_E$ | $\mathbf{1}_E$ | Indicator variable $\mathbf{1}_A$ | $\mathbf{1}_A$ |
| Lebesgue integral $\int f \, d\mu$ | $\int_X f \, d\mu$ | Mathematical Expectation | $\mathbb{E}[X]$ |
| Almost everywhere ($a.e.$) | a.e. | Almost surely ($a.s.$) | a.s. |
| Convergence in measure | $f_n \xrightarrow{\mu} f$ | Convergence in probability | $X_n \xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}} X$ |
| \(L^1\)-norm convergence | $\lVert f_n - f \rVert_1 \to 0$ | Convergence in mean | $\mathbb{E}[\lvert X_n - X \rvert] \to 0$ |
| Product measure | $\mu \otimes \nu$ | Joint distribution of independent variables | $\mathbb{P}_X \otimes \mathbb{P}_Y$ |
