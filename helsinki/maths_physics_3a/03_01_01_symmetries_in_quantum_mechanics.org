* 1. The Mathematical Framework of Quantum Mechanics

** 1.1 Hilbert Space $\mathcal{H}$
In quantum mechanics, the state of a physical system is described by a vector in a *Hilbert space* $\mathcal{H}$:
- $\mathcal{H}$ is a complex vector space equipped with a positive-definite Hermitian inner product $\langle \cdot \mid \cdot \rangle$.
- $\mathcal{H}$ is *complete* with respect to the norm induced by the inner product $\|\psi\| = \sqrt{\langle\psi \mid \psi\rangle}$ (every Cauchy sequence converges to a limit inside $\mathcal{H}$).
- State vectors are denoted using Dirac ket notation: $|\psi\rangle \in \mathcal{H}$.
- Dual vectors are denoted by bras: $\langle\phi| \in \mathcal{H}^*$.

** 1.2 Ray Representation of Physical States
In quantum mechanics, physical observables depend only on probability densities and transition probabilities:
$$ P(\phi \to \psi) = \frac{|\langle\phi \mid \psi\rangle|^2}{\langle\phi \mid \phi\rangle \langle\psi \mid \psi\rangle} $$
Multiplying a state vector by an arbitrary global phase factor leaves all physical predictions unchanged:
$$ |\psi\rangle \sim e^{i\theta} |\psi\rangle, \quad \theta \in [0, 2\pi) $$
Therefore, a pure physical state corresponds not to an individual vector, but to a 1-dimensional ray in Hilbert space:
$$ \underline{\psi} = \{ c |\psi\rangle \mid c \in \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\} \} \in \mathbb{P}\mathcal{H} $$
This formulation is known as the *ray representation* of quantum physical states.

** 1.3 Quantum Dynamics: The Schrödinger Equation
The time evolution of a quantum state is governed by the *Schrödinger equation*:
$$ i\hbar \frac{d}{dt} |\psi(t)\rangle = H |\psi(t)\rangle $$
where $H$ is the *Hamiltonian operator* (energy observable).
Because probabilities must be conserved over time ($\frac{d}{dt}\langle\psi(t)\mid\psi(t)\rangle = 0$), $H$ must be a self-adjoint (Hermitian) operator:
$$ H^\dagger = H $$

* 2. Symmetries in Quantum Mechanics

** 2.1 Definition of a Symmetry Transformation
A physical system possesses a symmetry under a transformation group $G$ if all measurable physical properties—specifically, transition probabilities—remain invariant under the transformations:
$$ |\langle U_g \phi \mid U_g \psi\rangle|^2 = |\langle\phi \mid \psi\rangle|^2, \quad \forall |\phi\rangle, |\psi\rangle \in \mathcal{H}, \, \forall g \in G $$
By *Wigner's Theorem*, any symmetry transformation on the space of rays can be lifted to an operator on $\mathcal{H}$ that is either:
1. *Unitary and linear:* $\langle U_g \phi \mid U_g \psi\rangle = \langle\phi \mid \psi\rangle$ (continuous symmetries like rotations, translations, Lorentz boosts).
2. *Antiunitary and antilinear:* $\langle U_g \phi \mid U_g \psi\rangle = \langle\phi \mid \psi\rangle^*$ (discrete time-reversal symmetry $\mathcal{T}$).

For continuous groups, we are exclusively concerned with *unitary representations*:
$$ g \mapsto U_g, \quad U_g^\dagger U_g = U_g U_g^\dagger = \mathbf{1}_\mathcal{H} $$

** 2.2 Invariance of Dynamics and Commutation with $H$
In a closed quantum system, the symmetry must also be preserved under time evolution.
Suppose $|\psi(t)\rangle$ is a solution to the Schrödinger equation:
$$ i\hbar \frac{d}{dt} |\psi(t)\rangle = H |\psi(t)\rangle $$
Apply the symmetry transformation to produce a new state $|\psi'(t)\rangle = U_g |\psi(t)\rangle$.
If $G$ is a symmetry of the system, $|\psi'(t)\rangle$ must also satisfy the Schrödinger equation:
$$ i\hbar \frac{d}{dt} (U_g |\psi(t)\rangle) = H (U_g |\psi(t)\rangle) $$
Assuming $U_g$ has no explicit time dependence:
$$ U_g \left( i\hbar \frac{d}{dt} |\psi(t)\rangle \right) = H U_g |\psi(t)\rangle $$
Substituting the original Schrödinger equation on the left-hand side:
$$ U_g H |\psi(t)\rangle = H U_g |\psi(t)\rangle $$
Since this holds for any state $|\psi(t)\rangle$, we conclude:
$$ U_g H = H U_g \iff [H, U_g] = 0 $$
Equivalently:
$$ U_g H U_g^{-1} = H $$

#+begin_important
*The Fundamental Symmetry Condition:*
A group $G$ is a symmetry group of a quantum system if and only if its unitary representation operators commute with the Hamiltonian:
$$ [H, U_g] = 0, \quad \forall g \in G $$
#+end_important

* 3. Energy Eigenspaces and Spectral Degeneracy

** 3.1 Stationary States and Eigenspaces
Consider the time-independent Schrödinger equation (energy eigenvalue problem):
$$ H |\phi_n\rangle = E_n |\phi_n\rangle $$
An energy level $E_n$ is said to be *degenerate* if there exist $k > 1$ linearly independent eigenstates $\{|\phi_{n,1}\rangle, |\phi_{n,2}\rangle, \dots, |\phi_{n,k}\rangle\}$ with the same energy $E_n$.
The set of all linear combinations forms the *eigenspace* $\mathcal{H}_n$:
$$ \mathcal{H}_n = \mathrm{span}\{ |\phi_{n,1}\rangle, \dots, |\phi_{n,k}\rangle \} \subset \mathcal{H} $$
$\mathcal{H}_n$ is a $k$-dimensional vector subspace of $\mathcal{H}$.

** 3.2 Action of Symmetry on Eigenspaces
Now apply the symmetry operator $U_g$ to an eigenstate $|\phi_n\rangle$:
$$ H (U_g |\phi_n\rangle) = U_g H |\phi_n\rangle = U_g (E_n |\phi_n\rangle) = E_n (U_g |\phi_n\rangle) $$
#+begin_quote
*Crucial Conclusion:*
The transformed state $U_g |\phi_n\rangle$ is *also* an eigenstate of $H$ with the *exact same energy eigenvalue* $E_n$!
#+end_quote

Therefore:
- The unitary operator $U_g$ maps the eigenspace $\mathcal{H}_n$ into itself:
  $$ U_g : \mathcal{H}_n \to \mathcal{H}_n $$
- The restriction $D^{(n)}(g) \equiv U_g|_{\mathcal{H}_n}$ forms a $k$-dimensional linear representation of the symmetry group $G$ acting on $\mathcal{H}_n$!

* 4. The Representation-Theoretic Origin of Degeneracy

** 4.1 Symmetry-Enforced Degeneracy
#+begin_important
*The Core Principle:*
If a quantum system possesses a symmetry group $G$, then the dimensions of the irreducible representations (irreps) of $G$ dictate the possible degeneracies of the energy spectrum of $H$.
#+end_important

- If $\mathcal{H}_n$ forms an *irreducible representation* of $G$ of dimension $k = \dim D$, then the $k$-fold degeneracy is *symmetry-enforced*: no perturbation that preserves the symmetry $G$ can lift this degeneracy!
- Examples:
  - *Spherical symmetry ($\mathrm{SO}(3)$ or $\mathrm{SU}(2)$):* The $(2j+1)$-fold degeneracy of angular momentum states $|j, m\rangle$ with $m = -j, \dots, +j$.
  - *Parity symmetry ($\mathbb{Z}_2$):* Energy eigenstates have definite parity (even or odd).
  - *Permutation symmetry ($S_N$):* Exchange degeneracy of $N$ identical particles (Slater determinants for fermions, symmetric states for bosons).

** 4.2 "Accidental" Degeneracy and Hidden Symmetries
When an energy eigenspace $\mathcal{H}_n$ decomposes into multiple irreducible representations of $G$, the degeneracy is historically termed *accidental*.
However, in theoretical physics, "accidental" degeneracies almost always signify a *larger, hidden dynamical symmetry group* that was not initially apparent:
1. *Hydrogen Atom Coulomb Potential ($V(r) \propto -1/r$):*
   The $n^2$-fold degeneracy of energy level $n$ (combining all orbital angular momenta $l = 0, 1, \dots, n-1$) is not explained by geometric spatial rotations $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ alone. It is enforced by the conservation of the *Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector*, generating a hidden dynamical $\mathrm{SO}(4)$ symmetry!
2. *3D Isotropic Harmonic Oscillator:*
   Possesses a hidden $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ dynamical symmetry explaining the $\frac{(N+1)(N+2)}{2}$-fold degeneracy of energy level $N$.
