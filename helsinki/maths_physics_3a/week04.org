* 1. Free Groups and Group Presentations (§2.7)

** 1.1 Free Groups $F(X)$
Let $X = \{g_1, g_2, \dots, g_n\}$ be a non-empty set of symbols called *letters* (or free generators).
- *Word:* Any formal finite sequence of letters and their formal inverses:
  $$ w = g_{j_1}^{i_1} g_{j_2}^{i_2} \cdots g_{j_m}^{i_m}, \quad i_k \in \mathbb{Z} $$
- *Reduced Word:* A word in which:
  1. No letter has exponent zero ($i_k \neq 0$).
  2. No two adjacent letters are identical ($g_{j_k} \neq g_{j_{k+1}}$).
  3. The empty word is denoted by $e$ (the identity element).
- *Reduction Algorithm:* If a word is not reduced, iteratively cancel adjacent pairs of inverses $g g^{-1} \to e$ and combine adjacent powers $g^p g^q \to g^{p+q}$ until a reduced word is obtained.
- *The Free Group $F(X)$:* The set of all reduced words formed from letters of $X$. Multiplication is concatenation followed by reduction.
  - For $X = \{a\}$: $F(\{a\}) = \{a^n \mid n \in \mathbb{Z}\} \cong (\mathbb{Z}, +)$.
  - For $|X| \ge 2$: $F(X)$ is non-Abelian and infinitely generated.

** 1.2 Group Presentations $\langle X \mid R \rangle$
In concrete groups, generators satisfy constraints called *relations*:
$$ r \equiv g_{j_1}^{i_1} g_{j_2}^{i_2} \cdots g_{j_m}^{i_m} = e $$
A group presentation $\langle X \mid R \rangle$ defines a group $G$ as the quotient of the free group $F(X)$ by the normal closure $N(R)$ of the relations $R$:
$$ G = \langle g_1, \dots, g_n \mid r_1 = e, \dots, r_m = e \rangle \cong F(X) / N(R) $$

*** Canonical Examples
1. *Cyclic Group $\mathbb{Z}_n$:*
   $$ \mathbb{Z}_n = \langle a \mid a^n = e \rangle $$
2. *Dihedral Group $D_4$ (Symmetries of a Square):*
   Generated by rotation $a$ (by $\pi/2$) and reflection $b$:
   $$ D_4 = \langle a, b \mid a^4 = e, \, b^2 = e, \, (ab)^2 = e \rangle $$
   Note that $(ab)^2 = e \iff bab = a^{-1} = a^3$.
3. *Free Abelian Group on Two Generators:*
   $$ \langle a, b \mid a b a^{-1} b^{-1} = e \rangle \cong (\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}, +) $$

** 1.3 Artin Braid Groups $B_n$ (§2.7.2)
Geometric braids consist of $n$ vertical strands hanging from top to bottom.
- *Generator $\sigma_i$ ($1 \le i \le n-1$):* Moves strand $i$ *over* strand $i+1$.
- *Inverse $\sigma_i^{-1}$:* Moves strand $i+1$ *over* strand $i$ (i.e., strand $i$ passes *under* strand $i+1$).
- *Re-indexing Rule:* After each crossing operation, the strands are re-indexed strictly from left to right as $1, 2, \dots, n$ before the next operation is applied.
- *Drawing Rule:* Read the letters of the braid word from *left to right*, performing the operations from *top to bottom*.
- *Artin Relations:*
  1. *Far Commutativity:*
     $$ \sigma_i \sigma_j = \sigma_j \sigma_i \quad \text{for } |i - j| \ge 2 $$
  2. *Third Reidemeister Move / Yang-Baxter Relation:*
     $$ \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i = \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \quad \text{for } 1 \le i \le n-2 $$
- *Presentation of $B_n$:*
  $$ B_n = \langle \sigma_1, \dots, \sigma_{n-1} \mid \sigma_i \sigma_j = \sigma_j \sigma_i \; (|i-j|\ge 2), \; \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i = \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \; (1 \le i \le n-2) \rangle $$
- *Connection to the Symmetric Group $S_n$:* Imposing $\sigma_i^2 = e$ yields $B_n / \langle \sigma_i^2 = e \rangle \cong S_n$.
- *Physical Applications:* Particle statistics in $(2+1)$D spacetime; anyons and topological quantum computation.

* 2. Complex Vector Spaces and Linear Operators (§3.1)

** 2.1 Complex Vector Spaces
A complex vector space $V$ is an Abelian group $(V, +)$ equipped with scalar multiplication by complex numbers $\mu \in \mathbb{C}$:
$$ \cdot : \mathbb{C} \times V \to V, \quad (\mu, \vec{v}) \mapsto \mu \vec{v} $$
satisfying the following axioms for all $\mu, \mu_1, \mu_2 \in \mathbb{C}$ and $\vec{v}, \vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2 \in V$:
- *VS1:* $\mu (\vec{v}_1 + \vec{v}_2) = \mu \vec{v}_1 + \mu \vec{v}_2$
- *VS2:* $(\mu_1 + \mu_2) \vec{v} = \mu_1 \vec{v} + \mu_2 \vec{v}$
- *VS3:* $\mu_1 (\mu_2 \vec{v}) = (\mu_1 \mu_2) \vec{v}$
- *VS4:* $1 \vec{v} = \vec{v}$
- *VS5:* $0 \vec{v} = \vec{0}$

** 2.2 Linear Independence, Basis, and Dimension
- *Linear Independence:* A set of vectors $\{\vec{v}_1, \dots, \vec{v}_k\}$ is linearly independent if:
  $$ \sum_{i=1}^k \mu_i \vec{v}_i = \vec{0} \implies \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \cdots = \mu_k = 0 $$
- *Basis:* A maximal linearly independent set $\{\vec{e}_1, \dots, \vec{e}_n\}$ that spans $V$.
- *Dimension:* The number of vectors in a basis: $\dim V = n$. Every vector $\vec{v} \in V$ has a unique coordinate expansion:
  $$ \vec{v} = \sum_{i=1}^n v_i \vec{e}_i $$

** 2.3 Linear Maps, Image, Kernel, and Automorphisms
A map $L : V_1 \to V_2$ is a linear map (or linear operator) if:
$$ L(\mu_1 \vec{v}_1 + \mu_2 \vec{v}_2) = \mu_1 L(\vec{v}_1) + \mu_2 L(\vec{v}_2), \quad \forall \mu_1, \mu_2 \in \mathbb{C}, \, \vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2 \in V_1 $$
- *Image:* $\mathrm{Im}(L) = \{ L(\vec{v}) \mid \vec{v} \in V_1 \} \subseteq V_2$ (a subspace of $V_2$).
- *Kernel:* $\mathrm{Ker}(L) = \{ \vec{v} \in V_1 \mid L(\vec{v}) = \vec{0}_2 \} \subseteq V_1$ (a subspace of $V_1$).

#+begin_quote
*Theorem 3.1 (Rank-Nullity Theorem):*
$$ \dim V_1 = \dim \mathrm{Ker}(L) + \dim \mathrm{Im}(L) $$
#+end_quote

#+begin_quote
*Theorem 3.2:*
A linear map $L : V \to V$ is an automorphism if and only if $\mathrm{Ker}(L) = \{\vec{0}\}$.
#+end_quote

- *Matrix Representation:* In a chosen basis, $L(\vec{e}_i) = \sum_{j=1}^n L^j_i \vec{e}_j$. The group of all linear automorphisms $\mathrm{Aut}(V)$ is isomorphic to the general linear group:
  $$ \mathrm{Aut}(V) \cong \mathrm{GL}(n, \mathbb{C}) \quad (\text{or } \mathrm{GL}(n, \mathbb{R}) \text{ if real}) $$

* 3. Group Representations (§3.1)

** 3.1 Definition of a Linear Representation
A *linear representation* $D$ of a group $G$ in a vector space $V$ is a group homomorphism:
$$ D : G \to \mathrm{Aut}(V), \quad g \mapsto D(g) $$
satisfying:
1. $D(g_1 g_2) = D(g_1) D(g_2)$ for all $g_1, g_2 \in G$.
2. $D(g^{-1}) = (D(g))^{-1}$.
3. $D(e) = \mathbf{1}_V$.
The *dimension* of the representation is $\dim D = \dim V$.

** 3.2 Faithful Representations
A representation $D$ is called *faithful* if the homomorphism $D$ is an injection:
$$ \mathrm{Ker}(D) = \{ g \in G \mid D(g) = \mathbf{1}_V \} = \{e\} $$
In a faithful representation, distinct group elements are represented by distinct operators ($g_1 \neq g_2 \implies D(g_1) \neq D(g_2)$).
- If $\mathrm{Ker}(D) \neq \{e\}$, the representation is *non-faithful*.
- If $D(g) = \mathbf{1}_V$ for all $g \in G$, $D$ is the *trivial representation*.

** 3.3 Intertwining Operators and Equivalence
Two representations $D_1 : G \to \mathrm{Aut}(V_1)$ and $D_2 : G \to \mathrm{Aut}(V_2)$ are *equivalent* ($D_1 \sim D_2$) if there exists an invertible linear operator (intertwining isomorphism) $A : V_1 \to V_2$ such that:
$$ D_2(g) A = A D_1(g) \iff D_2(g) = A D_1(g) A^{-1}, \quad \forall g \in G $$
Equivalence corresponds to expressing the same abstract representation in different bases.

* 4. Inner Product Spaces and Unitary Representations (§3.1)

** 4.1 Hermitian Scalar Product
A scalar product on a complex vector space $V$ is a map $\langle \cdot \mid \cdot \rangle : V \times V \to \mathbb{C}$ such that:
1. $\langle \vec{v} \mid \mu_1 \vec{w}_1 + \mu_2 \vec{w}_2 \rangle = \mu_1 \langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{w}_1 \rangle + \mu_2 \langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{w}_2 \rangle$ (linear in 2nd argument).
2. $\langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{w} \rangle = \langle \vec{w} \mid \vec{v} \rangle^*$ (conjugate symmetric).
3. $\langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{v} \rangle \ge 0$, and $\langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{v} \rangle = 0 \iff \vec{v} = \vec{0}$ (positive definite).

** 4.2 Gram-Schmidt Orthonormalization
Given an arbitrary linearly independent basis $\{\vec{u}_1, \dots, \vec{u}_n\}$, an orthonormal basis $\{\vec{e}_1, \dots, \vec{e}_n\}$ ($\langle \vec{e}_i \mid \vec{e}_j \rangle = \delta_{ij}$) is constructed by:
$$ \vec{e}_1 = \frac{\vec{u}_1}{\|\vec{u}_1\|}, \quad \vec{w}_i = \vec{u}_i - \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \langle \vec{e}_j \mid \vec{u}_i \rangle \vec{e}_j, \quad \vec{e}_i = \frac{\vec{w}_i}{\|\vec{w}_i\|} $$

** 4.3 Unitary Operators and Unitary Representations
- *Adjoint $A^\dagger$:* Defined by $\langle \vec{v} \mid A^\dagger \vec{w} \rangle = \langle A\vec{v} \mid \vec{w} \rangle$.
- *Unitary Operator $U$:* Preserves the inner product:
  $$ \langle U\vec{v} \mid U\vec{w} \rangle = \langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{w} \rangle \iff U^\dagger U = \mathbf{1}_V $$
  Unitary operators form the subgroup $\mathrm{Unit}(V) \cong \mathrm{U}(n) \subset \mathrm{Aut}(V)$.
- *Unitary Representation:* A homomorphism $D : G \to \mathrm{Unit}(V)$.
- *Unitary Equivalence:* Two unitary representations $U_1, U_2$ are unitarily equivalent if their intertwining operator $A$ is unitary ($A^\dagger A = \mathbf{1}$):
  $$ U_2(g) = A U_1(g) A^\dagger, \quad \forall g \in G $$

* 5. Symmetries in Quantum Mechanics (§3.1.1)

In quantum mechanics, physical states are rays in a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$, and time evolution is generated by the Hamiltonian $H$:
$$ i\hbar \frac{d}{dt}|\psi(t)\rangle = H |\psi(t)\rangle $$
- *Symmetry Condition:* A group $G$ is a symmetry group of a quantum system if its unitary representation $g \mapsto U_g$ preserves transition probabilities and commutes with time evolution:
  $$ [H, U_g] = 0 \iff U_g H U_g^{-1} = H, \quad \forall g \in G $$
- *Spectral Degeneracy:* Let $H |\phi_n\rangle = E_n |\phi_n\rangle$. Because $[H, U_g] = 0$:
  $$ H (U_g |\phi_n\rangle) = U_g H |\phi_n\rangle = E_n (U_g |\phi_n\rangle) $$
  $U_g$ maps the energy eigenspace $\mathcal{H}_n$ into itself.
  Therefore, *each eigenspace forms a carrier space for a representation of the symmetry group $G$*.
  *The dimensions of the irreducible representations of $G$ dictate the possible degeneracies of the energy levels!*

* 6. Direct Sums and Tensor Products of Vector Spaces (§3.6.2)

** 6.1 Direct Sum $V \oplus W$
Given vector spaces $V$ and $W$ over $\mathbb{C}$ (or $\mathbb{R}$) with dimensions $M$ and $N$:
- The *external direct sum* is:
  $$ V \oplus W = \{ (v, w) \mid v \in V, \, w \in W \} $$
  with operations $(v_1, w_1) + (v_2, w_2) = (v_1 + v_2, w_1 + w_2)$ and $\mu(v, w) = (\mu v, \mu w)$.
- If $\{v_1, \dots, v_M\}$ is a basis of $V$ and $\{w_1, \dots, w_N\}$ is a basis of $W$, then:
  $$ \mathcal{B}_{\oplus} = \{ (v_i, 0) \}_{i=1}^M \cup \{ (0, w_j) \}_{j=1}^N $$
  forms a basis of $V \oplus W$.
- Consequently:
  $$ \dim(V \oplus W) = \dim V + \dim W = M + N $$

** 6.2 Tensor Product $V \otimes W$
- The *tensor product* $V \otimes W$ is the vector space spanned by simple product elements $v \otimes w$ subject to bilinearity relations:
  - $(v_1 + v_2) \otimes w = v_1 \otimes w + v_2 \otimes w$
  - $v \otimes (w_1 + w_2) = v \otimes w_1 + v \otimes w_2$
  - $(\mu v) \otimes w = v \otimes (\mu w) = \mu(v \otimes w)$
- If $\{v_i\}_{i=1}^M$ and $\{w_j\}_{j=1}^N$ are bases of $V$ and $W$, the set of simple tensors:
  $$ \mathcal{B}_{\otimes} = \{ v_i \otimes w_j \mid 1 \le i \le M, \, 1 \le j \le N \} $$
  forms a basis of $V \otimes W$.
- Consequently:
  $$ \dim(V \otimes W) = (\dim V)(\dim W) = M \cdot N $$
