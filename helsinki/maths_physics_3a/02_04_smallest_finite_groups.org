* 1. Overview and Physical Context

Finite groups play a foundational role in physics:
- *Solid-State Physics:* Classification of the 32 crystallographic point groups and 230 space groups characterizing crystal lattices.
- *Quantum Mechanics:* Selection rules, multiplet structures, and degeneracy of atomic/molecular orbitals.
- *Classical Mechanics:* Reduction of dynamical degrees of freedom in symmetric mechanical systems.

For small orders $N \le 8$, the group axioms constrain the multiplication laws so rigidly that all possible abstract groups can be exhaustively classified and tabulated.

* 3. Orders 1 through 3

** 3.1 Order $N = 1$: The Trivial Group $\mathbb{Z}_1$
Contains only the identity element: $G = \{e\}$ with $e \cdot e = e$.

** 3.2 Order $N = 2$: The Cyclic Group $\mathbb{Z}_2$
Let $G = \{e, a\}$ with $a \neq e$.
Closure requires $a^2 \in \{e, a\}$. If $a^2 = a$, cancellation implies $a = e$ (contradiction). Thus $a^2 = e$.
Realization: $\mathbb{Z}_2 \cong S_2 = \{E, (12)\}$.

#+caption: Cayley table for $\mathbb{Z}_2$
| $\cdot$ | $e$ | $a$ |
|---------+-----+-----|
| $e$     | $e$ | $a$ |
| $a$     | $a$ | $e$ |

** 3.3 Order $N = 3$: The Cyclic Group $\mathbb{Z}_3$
Since $3$ is prime, by Lagrange's corollary $G \cong \mathbb{Z}_3 = \{e, a, a^2\}$ with $a^3 = e$.
Inverses: $a^{-1} = a^2$, $(a^2)^{-1} = a$.

#+caption: Cayley table for $\mathbb{Z}_3$
| $\cdot$ | $e$   | $a$   | $a^2$ |
|---------+-------+-------+-------|
| $e$     | $e$   | $a$   | $a^2$ |
| $a$     | $a$   | $a^2$ | $e$   |
| $a^2$   | $a^2$ | $e$   | $a$   |

* 4. The General Cyclic Group $\mathbb{Z}_N$

A *cyclic group* is a group generated by powers of a single element $a$:
$$ \mathbb{Z}_N = \{e, a, a^2, \dots, a^{N-1}\}, \quad a^N = e $$
- Inverses: $(a^p)^{-1} = a^{N-p}$.
- Commutativity: $a^p a^q = a^{p+q \pmod N} = a^q a^p \implies$ all cyclic groups are Abelian.
- *Realizations:*
  1. Roots of unity: $\mathbb{Z}_N \cong \{ e^{2\pi i k / N} \mid k = 0, 1, \dots, N-1 \} \subset \mathbb{C}^\times$.
  2. Geometry: Rotational symmetries of an oriented regular $N$-sided polygon.
  3. Integers modulo $N$: $(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}, +)$.

* 5. Order $N = 4$: $\mathbb{Z}_4$ and the Klein Four-Group $V_4$

There are exactly two non-isomorphic groups of order 4:

** 5.1 Cyclic Group $\mathbb{Z}_4$
$G = \{e, a, a^2, a^3\}$ with $a^4 = e$. Elements have orders: $|e|=1$, $|a|=4$, $|a^2|=2$, $|a^3|=4$.

#+caption: Cayley table for $\mathbb{Z}_4$
| $\cdot$ | $e$   | $a$   | $a^2$ | $a^3$ |
|---------+-------+-------+-------+-------|
| $e$     | $e$   | $a$   | $a^2$ | $a^3$ |
| $a$     | $a$   | $a^2$ | $a^3$ | $e$   |
| $a^2$   | $a^2$ | $a^3$ | $e$   | $a$   |
| $a^3$   | $a^3$ | $e$   | $a$   | $a^2$ |

** 5.2 The Klein Four-Group $V_4 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$
Named *Vierergruppe* by Christian Felix Klein.
Let $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2 = \{e_1, b\} \times \{e_2, c\}$. Denoting $e = (e_1, e_2)$, $x = (b, e_2)$, $y = (e_1, c)$, $z = (b, c)$:
Every non-identity element has order 2:
$$ x^2 = y^2 = z^2 = e, \quad xy = yx = z, \quad yz = zy = x, \quad zx = xz = y $$
$V_4$ is the *smallest non-cyclic group*.

#+caption: Cayley table for $V_4$
| $\cdot$ | $e$ | $x$ | $y$ | $z$ |
|---------+-----+-----+-----+-----|
| $e$     | $e$ | $x$ | $y$ | $z$ |
| $x$     | $x$ | $e$ | $z$ | $y$ |
| $y$     | $y$ | $z$ | $e$ | $x$ |
| $z$     | $z$ | $y$ | $x$ | $e$ |

* 6. Order $N = 6$: $\mathbb{Z}_6$ and the Symmetric Group $S_3$

** 6.1 The Cyclic Group $\mathbb{Z}_6 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_3$
Since $\gcd(2, 3) = 1$, by the Chinese Remainder Theorem $\mathbb{Z}_6 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_3$.
An explicit isomorphism mapping $(b^p, c^q) \in \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_3$ to $a^k \in \mathbb{Z}_6$ is:
$$ (b^p, c^q) \mapsto a^{(3p + 4q) \pmod 6} $$
For example: $(e_1, c) \mapsto a^4$, $(b, c^2) \mapsto a^5$, and $(e_1, c) \cdot (b, c^2) = (b, e_2) \mapsto a^3$, matching $a^4 \cdot a^5 = a^9 \equiv a^3$.

** 6.2 The Symmetric Group $S_3 \cong D_3$ (The Smallest Non-Abelian Group)
$S_3$ is the group of permutations of 3 objects ($|S_3| = 3! = 6$), which also represents the symmetry group $D_3$ of an equilateral triangle.
Generators: $a$ (reflection) and $b$ (rotation by $2\pi/3$).
Relations: $a^2 = b^2 = (ab)^3 = e \implies aba = bab$.
Elements: $\{e, a, b, aba, ab, ba\}$.

#+caption: Cayley table for $S_3$ (Note asymmetry across main diagonal $\implies$ non-Abelian)
| $\cdot$ | $e$     | $a$     | $b$     | $aba$   | $ab$    | $ba$    |
|---------+---------+---------+---------+---------+---------+---------|
| $e$     | $e$     | $a$     | $b$     | $aba$   | $ab$    | $ba$    |
| $a$     | $a$     | $e$     | $ba$    | $ab$    | $aba$   | $b$     |
| $b$     | $b$     | $ab$    | $e$     | $ba$    | $a$     | $aba$   |
| $aba$   | $aba$   | $ba$    | $ab$    | $e$     | $b$     | $a$     |
| $ab$    | $ab$    | $b$     | $aba$   | $a$     | $ba$    | $e$     |
| $ba$    | $ba$    | $aba$   | $a$     | $b$     | $e$     | $ab$    |

* 7. Order $N = 8$: The Five Groups of Order 8

There are exactly 5 groups of order 8: 3 Abelian and 2 Non-Abelian.

** 7.1 Abelian Groups of Order 8
1. $\mathbb{Z}_8$ (cyclic group of order 8).
2. $\mathbb{Z}_4 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$.
3. $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ (elementary Abelian 2-group).

** 7.2 The Dihedral Group $D_4$ (Symmetries of the Square)
Symmetries of a square: 4 rotations (multiples of $\pi/2$) and 4 reflections (2 across side bisectors, 2 across diagonals).
Elements: $\{e, a, a^2, a^3, b, ab, a^2b, a^3b\}$ where $a^4 = e$, $b^2 = e$, $bab = a^{-1} = a^3$.

#+caption: Cayley table for $D_4$
| $\cdot$ | $e$    | $a$    | $a^2$  | $a^3$  | $b$    | $ab$   | $a^2b$ | $a^3b$ |
|---------+--------+--------+--------+--------+--------+--------+--------+--------|
| $e$     | $e$    | $a$    | $a^2$  | $a^3$  | $b$    | $ab$   | $a^2b$ | $a^3b$ |
| $a$     | $a$    | $a^2$  | $a^3$  | $e$    | $ab$   | $a^2b$ | $a^3b$ | $b$    |
| $a^2$   | $a^2$  | $a^3$  | $e$    | $a$    | $a^2b$ | $a^3b$ | $b$    | $ab$   |
| $a^3$   | $a^3$  | $e$    | $a$    | $a^2$  | $a^3b$ | $b$    | $ab$   | $a^2b$ |
| $b$     | $b$    | $a^3b$ | $a^2b$ | $ab$   | $e$    | $a^3$  | $a^2$  | $a$    |
| $ab$    | $ab$   | $b$    | $a^3b$ | $a^2b$ | $a$    | $e$    | $a^3$  | $a^2$  |
| $a^2b$  | $a^2b$ | $ab$   | $b$    | $a^3b$ | $a^2$  | $a$    | $e$    | $a^3$  |
| $a^3b$  | $a^3b$ | $a^2b$ | $ab$   | $b$    | $a^3$  | $a^2$  | $a$    | $e$    |

- *Center of $D_4$:* $Z(D_4) = \{e, a^2\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$.
- *Quotient:* $D_4 / Z(D_4) \cong V_4 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$.

** 7.3 The Quaternion Group $Q_8$
$Q_8 = \{ \pm e, \pm i, \pm j, \pm k \}$ with:
$$ i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = ijk = -e $$
Multiplication rules:
$$ ij = k = -ji, \quad jk = i = -kj, \quad ki = j = -ik $$

#+caption: Cayley table for $Q_8$
| $\cdot$ | $e$  | $-e$ | $i$  | $-i$ | $j$  | $-j$ | $k$  | $-k$ |
|---------+------+------+------+------+------+------+------+------|
| $e$     | $e$  | $-e$ | $i$  | $-i$ | $j$  | $-j$ | $k$  | $-k$ |
| $-e$    | $-e$ | $e$  | $-i$ | $i$  | $-j$ | $j$  | $-k$ | $k$  |
| $i$     | $i$  | $-i$ | $-e$ | $e$  | $k$  | $-k$ | $-j$ | $j$  |
| $-i$    | $-i$ | $i$  | $e$  | $-e$ | $-k$ | $k$  | $j$  | $-j$ |
| $j$     | $j$  | $-j$ | $-k$ | $k$  | $-e$ | $e$  | $i$  | $-i$ |
| $-j$    | $-j$ | $j$  | $k$  | $-k$ | $e$  | $-e$ | $-i$ | $i$  |
| $k$     | $k$  | $-k$ | $j$  | $-j$ | $-i$ | $i$  | $-e$ | $e$  |
| $-k$    | $-k$ | $k$  | $-j$ | $j$  | $i$  | $-i$ | $e$  | $-e$ |

*** Subgroup Structure of $Q_8$
$Q_8$ has four proper subgroups:
- $Z(Q_8) = \{e, -e\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$ (the center).
- Three cyclic subgroups of order 4:
  $$ H_i = \{e, i, -e, -i\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_4, \quad H_j = \{e, j, -e, -j\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_4, \quad H_k = \{e, k, -e, -k\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_4 $$
*Remarkable Property:*
*Every single subgroup of $Q_8$ is normal!*
A non-Abelian group with this property is called a *Hamiltonian group* (named after Sir William Rowan Hamilton).
Quotient group: $Q_8 / Z(Q_8) \cong V_4 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$.
