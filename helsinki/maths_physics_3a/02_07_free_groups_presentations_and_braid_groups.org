* 1. Free Groups and Group Presentations

** 1.1 Free Groups
Let $X = \{g_1, g_2, \dots, g_n\}$ be a non-empty set of symbols called *letters* (or free generators).
- A *word* is an arbitrary finite sequence formed from the letters and their formal inverses:
  $$ w = g_{j_1}^{i_1} g_{j_2}^{i_2} \cdots g_{j_m}^{i_m}, \quad i_k \in \mathbb{Z} $$
- A word is a *reduced word* if:
  1. No exponent is zero ($i_k \neq 0$).
  2. No two adjacent letters are identical ($g_{j_k} \neq g_{j_{k+1}}$).
  3. The empty sequence is denoted by $e$ (the identity).
- The *free group generated by $X$*, denoted $F(X)$, is the set of all reduced words.
- *Group product:* Concatenation of words followed by reduction (iteratively cancelling adjacent inverses $g g^{-1} \to e$).

*** Examples
- $X = \{a\}$: The free group is $F(\{a\}) = \{a^n \mid n \in \mathbb{Z}\} \cong (\mathbb{Z}, +)$.
- $X = \{a, b\}$: $F(\{a, b\}) = \{e, a^n, b^m, a^n b^m, b^m a^n, a^n b^m a^k, \dots\}$ is an infinite, highly non-Abelian group.

** 1.2 Group Presentations
In concrete groups, generators satisfy constraints called *relations*:
$$ r \equiv g_{j_1}^{i_1} g_{j_2}^{i_2} \cdots g_{j_m}^{i_m} = e $$
A *presentation* of a group $G$ consists of a set of generators $X$ and a set of relations $R = \{r_1, r_2, \dots, r_m\}$:
$$ G = \langle X \mid R \rangle \equiv \langle g_1, \dots, g_n \mid r_1 = e, \dots, r_m = e \rangle $$
Formally, $G$ is isomorphic to the quotient of the free group $F(X)$ by the normal closure $N(R)$ of the relations:
$$ G \cong F(X) / N(R) $$

*** Canonical Examples
1. *Cyclic Group $\mathbb{Z}_n$:*
   $$ \mathbb{Z}_n = \langle a \mid a^n = e \rangle $$
2. *Dihedral Group $D_n$:*
   Generated by rotation $r$ and reflection $s$:
   $$ D_n = \langle r, s \mid r^n = e, \, s^2 = e, \, s r s = r^{-1} \rangle $$
   For $D_4$ (symmetries of the square):
   $$ D_4 = \langle a, b \mid a^4 = e, \, b^2 = e, \, (ab)^2 = e \rangle $$
3. *Pauli Group $G_1$ (Order 16):*
   Consisting of the three Pauli matrices and phases $\{\pm \mathbf{1}, \pm i\mathbf{1}, \pm \sigma_x, \pm i\sigma_x, \pm \sigma_y, \pm i\sigma_y, \pm \sigma_z, \pm i\sigma_z\}$.
   Presentation:
   $$ G_1 = \langle \sigma_x, \sigma_z, i\mathbf{1} \mid \sigma_x^2 = \mathbf{1}, \, \sigma_z^2 = \mathbf{1}, \, (i\mathbf{1})^4 = \mathbf{1}, \, \sigma_x \sigma_z = -\sigma_z \sigma_x \rangle $$

* 2. The Artin Braid Group $B_n$

** 2.1 Geometric Definition
The *braid group on $n$ strands*, denoted $B_n$, was introduced by Emil Artin in 1925.
Consider $n$ vertical strands fixed at $n$ points at the top and $n$ points at the bottom.
- *Generators $\sigma_i$ ($1 \le i \le n-1$):*
  The elementary operation $\sigma_i$ passes strand $i$ *over* strand $i+1$.
- *Inverse $\sigma_i^{-1}$:*
  Passes strand $i$ *under* strand $i+1$.
- *Product of braids:*
  Concatenating two braids vertically (attaching the bottom of the first braid to the top of the second braid).

** 2.2 Artin Braid Relations
Deforming strands without cutting or passing through each other gives rise to two fundamental topological relations:

1. *Far Commutativity:*
   Braiding operations that affect disjoint pairs of strands are independent:
   $$ \sigma_i \sigma_j = \sigma_j \sigma_i \quad \text{for } |i - j| \ge 2 $$

2. *Third Reidemeister Move / Yang-Baxter Relation:*
   For three adjacent strands, sliding strand 1 past the crossing of strands 2 and 3:
   $$ \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i = \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \quad \text{for } 1 \le i \le n-2 $$

** 2.3 Presentation of $B_n$
The braid group $B_n$ is uniquely specified by the presentation:
$$ B_n = \langle \sigma_1, \sigma_2, \dots, \sigma_{n-1} \mid \sigma_i \sigma_j = \sigma_j \sigma_i \; (|i-j| \ge 2), \quad \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i = \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \; (1 \le i \le n-2) \rangle $$

** 2.4 Mathematical Properties
1. $B_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}_1$ (trivial group).
2. $B_2 \cong (\mathbb{Z}, +)$ (Abelian group parameterized by net winding number).
3. $B_3$ is non-Abelian. It is isomorphic to the fundamental group of the complement of the *trefoil knot* $K$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$:
   $$ B_3 \cong \pi_1(\mathbb{R}^3 \setminus K) $$
4. Nested sequence: $B_1 \subset B_2 \subset B_3 \subset \cdots \subset B_n \subset B_{n+1}$.
5. *Connection to the Symmetric Group $S_n$:*
   If we impose the additional constraint that over-crossing equals under-crossing ($\sigma_i = \sigma_i^{-1} \iff \sigma_i^2 = e$), we recover $S_n$:
   $$ S_n \cong B_n / \langle \sigma_i^2 = e \rangle $$

* 3. Physical Applications: Anyons and Quantum Computation

** 3.1 Statistics in 3D vs. 2D
- *In 3 (or higher) spatial dimensions:*
  Exchanging two identical particles twice traces a closed loop in configuration space. In $d \ge 3$, this loop can be continuously shrunk to a point without intersecting the other particle ($\pi_1 = \{e\}$). Thus exchanging particles twice must leave the wave function invariant:
  $$ \psi(x_1, x_2) \to e^{i\theta} \psi(x_2, x_1) \to e^{2i\theta} \psi(x_1, x_2) \implies e^{2i\theta} = 1 \implies e^{i\theta} = \pm 1 $$
  This leads strictly to *Bosons* ($+1$) and *Fermions* ($-1$), governed by representations of the symmetric group $S_n$.
- *In 2 spatial dimensions:*
  A particle cannot be looped around another and shrunk to a point without passing through the puncture ($\pi_1(\mathbb{R}^2 \setminus \{0\}) = \mathbb{Z}$). The particle trajectories form *braids* in $(2+1)$-dimensional spacetime!
  The exchange statistics is governed by the *braid group $B_n$*.
  Particles whose exchange yields an arbitrary phase $e^{i\theta}$ are called *anyons* (Frank Wilczek).

** 3.2 Non-Abelian Anyons and Topological Quantum Computing
When the ground state of a 2D topological phase is degenerate, particle exchange acts as a unitary matrix representation of $B_n$ on the ground-state subspace:
$$ |\psi_a\rangle \to \sum_b [D(\sigma_i)]_{ab} |\psi_b\rangle $$
Because $[D(\sigma_i), D(\sigma_{i+1})] \neq 0$, these particles are *non-Abelian anyons* (e.g., Majorana zero modes in Fractional Quantum Hall states at $\nu = 5/2$).
Braiding non-Abelian anyons performs unitary quantum gates whose accuracy is topologically protected against local environmental decoherence.
