* Complex Vector Spaces

** Axiomatic Definition
A *complex vector space* $V$ is an Abelian group $(V, +)$ (with additive identity $\vec{0}$) equipped with scalar multiplication by complex numbers $\mu \in \mathbb{C}$:
$$ \cdot : \mathbb{C} \times V \to V, \quad (\mu, \vec{v}) \mapsto \mu \vec{v} $$
satisfying the following axioms for all $\mu, \mu_1, \mu_2 \in \mathbb{C}$ and $\vec{v}, \vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2 \in V$:
- *VS1:* $\mu (\vec{v}_1 + \vec{v}_2) = \mu \vec{v}_1 + \mu \vec{v}_2$
- *VS2:* $(\mu_1 + \mu_2) \vec{v} = \mu_1 \vec{v} + \mu_2 \vec{v}$
- *VS3:* $\mu_1 (\mu_2 \vec{v}) = (\mu_1 \mu_2) \vec{v}$
- *VS4:* $1 \vec{v} = \vec{v}$
- *VS5:* $0 \vec{v} = \vec{0}$

** Linear Independence, Dimension, and Bases
- A set of vectors $\{\vec{v}_1, \dots, \vec{v}_k\}$ is *linearly independent* if:
  $$ \sum_{i=1}^k \mu_i \vec{v}_i = \vec{0} \implies \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \cdots = \mu_k = 0 $$
- The *dimension* of $V$, denoted $\dim V$, is the maximum number of linearly independent vectors in $V$.
- If $\dim V = n$, any set of $n$ linearly independent vectors $\{\vec{e}_1, \dots, \vec{e}_n\}$ forms a *basis*.
  Every vector $\vec{v} \in V$ has a unique expansion:
  $$ \vec{v} = \sum_{i=1}^n v_i \vec{e}_i $$

* Linear Maps and Automorphisms

** Linear Maps
A map $L : V_1 \to V_2$ between vector spaces is a *linear map* (or *linear operator*) if:
$$ L(\mu_1 \vec{v}_1 + \mu_2 \vec{v}_2) = \mu_1 L(\vec{v}_1) + \mu_2 L(\vec{v}_2), \quad \forall \mu_1, \mu_2 \in \mathbb{C}, \, \vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2 \in V_1 $$
- *Isomorphism:* A bijective linear map. If $V_1 \cong V_2$, then $\dim V_1 = \dim V_2$.
- *Automorphism:* An isomorphism from $V$ to itself ($L : V \to V$).
  The set of all automorphisms forms the *automorphism group* $\mathrm{Aut}(V)$ under map composition.

** Kernel, Image, and Fundamental Theorems
- *Image:* $\mathrm{Im}(L) = \{ L(\vec{v}) \mid \vec{v} \in V_1 \} \subseteq V_2$ (a subspace of $V_2$).
- *Kernel:* $\mathrm{Ker}(L) = \{ \vec{v} \in V_1 \mid L(\vec{v}) = \vec{0}_2 \} \subseteq V_1$ (a subspace of $V_1$).

#+begin_quote
*Theorem 3.1 (Rank-Nullity Theorem):*
$$ \dim V_1 = \dim \mathrm{Ker}(L) + \dim \mathrm{Im}(L) $$
#+end_quote

#+begin_quote
*Theorem 3.2:*
A linear map $L : V \to V$ is an automorphism if and only if $\mathrm{Ker}(L) = \{\vec{0}\}$.
#+end_quote

** Matrix Representation of Linear Operators
Given a basis $\{\vec{e}_1, \dots, \vec{e}_n\}$, the action of $L$ is uniquely fixed by its action on the basis vectors:
$$ L(\vec{e}_i) = \sum_{j=1}^n L^j_i \vec{e}_j $$
The components of the image $\vec{w} = L(\vec{v})$ are:
$$ w_j = \sum_{i=1}^n L^j_i v_i \iff \begin{pmatrix} w_1 \\ \vdots \\ w_n \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} L^1_1 & \cdots & L^1_n \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ L^n_1 & \cdots & L^n_n \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} v_1 \\ \vdots \\ v_n \end{pmatrix} $$
Thus, for $\dim V = n$:
$$ \mathrm{Aut}(V) \cong \mathrm{GL}(n, \mathbb{C}) $$

* Group Representations

** Definition of a Linear Representation
A *linear representation* $D$ of a group $G$ in a vector space $V$ is a group homomorphism:
$$ D : G \to \mathrm{Aut}(V), \quad g \mapsto D(g) $$
Properties:
1. $D(g_1 g_2) = D(g_1) D(g_2)$ for all $g_1, g_2 \in G$.
2. $D(g^{-1}) = (D(g))^{-1}$.
3. $D(e) = \mathbf{1}_V$ (identity operator).
The *dimension of the representation* is the dimension of the carrier vector space: $\dim D = \dim V$.

** Faithful Representations
A representation $D$ is called *faithful* if the homomorphism $D$ is an injection:
$$ \mathrm{Ker}(D) = \{ g \in G \mid D(g) = \mathbf{1}_V \} = \{e\} $$
In a faithful representation, distinct group elements are represented by distinct linear operators ($g_1 \neq g_2 \implies D(g_1) \neq D(g_2)$).

** Example: Representation of $\mathbb{Z}_4$ in $\mathbb{R}^2$
Let $\mathbb{Z}_4 = \{e, c, c^2, c^3\}$ where $c^4 = e$. In $V = \mathbb{R}^2$:
$$ D(c) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad D(c^2) = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 \end{pmatrix} = -\mathbf{1}_2 $$
$$ D(c^3) = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad D(e) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{1}_2 $$
$D(c)$ represents a counterclockwise rotation by $\pi/2$ in the plane.

* Intertwining Operators and Equivalence of Representations

** Intertwining Operators
Let $D_1 : G \to \mathrm{Aut}(V_1)$ and $D_2 : G \to \mathrm{Aut}(V_2)$ be two representations.
A linear map $A : V_1 \to V_2$ is called an *intertwining operator* (or *G-morphism*) if:
$$ D_2(g) A = A D_1(g), \quad \forall g \in G $$

** Equivalent Representations
If the intertwining operator $A$ is an isomorphism (requiring $\dim V_1 = \dim V_2$), then $D_1$ and $D_2$ are said to be *equivalent* ($D_1 \sim D_2$):
$$ D_2(g) = A D_1(g) A^{-1}, \quad \forall g \in G $$
Equivalent representations correspond to the same abstract representation expressed in different choices of basis.
*Consequence:* Any $n$-dimensional complex representation is equivalent to a representation by $n \times n$ invertible matrices $D : G \to \mathrm{GL}(n, \mathbb{C})$.

* Scalar Products and Gram-Schmidt Orthonormalization

** The Hermitian Scalar Product
A *scalar product* (Hermitian inner product) on $V$ is a map $\langle \cdot \mid \cdot \rangle : V \times V \to \mathbb{C}$ satisfying:
1. *Linearity in the second argument:*
   $$ \langle \vec{v} \mid \mu_1 \vec{v}_1 + \mu_2 \vec{v}_2 \rangle = \mu_1 \langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{v}_1 \rangle + \mu_2 \langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{v}_2 \rangle $$
2. *Conjugate symmetry:*
   $$ \langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{w} \rangle = \langle \vec{w} \mid \vec{v} \rangle^* $$
   (Combining 1 and 2 yields antilinearity in the first argument: $\langle \mu \vec{v} \mid \vec{w} \rangle = \mu^* \langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{w} \rangle$).
3. *Positive definiteness:*
   $$ \langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{v} \rangle \ge 0, \quad \text{and } \langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{v} \rangle = 0 \iff \vec{v} = \vec{0} $$

** Gram-Schmidt Orthonormalization Algorithm
Given an arbitrary linearly independent basis $\{\vec{u}_1, \vec{u}_2, \dots, \vec{u}_n\}$, an orthonormal basis $\{\vec{e}_1, \dots, \vec{e}_n\}$ ($\langle \vec{e}_i \mid \vec{e}_j \rangle = \delta_{ij}$) is constructed as follows:
1. Normalize the first vector:
   $$ \vec{e}_1 = \frac{\vec{u}_1}{\sqrt{\langle \vec{u}_1 \mid \vec{u}_1 \rangle}} $$
2. For $i = 2, 3, \dots, n$, project out components along already constructed basis vectors:
   $$ \vec{w}_i = \vec{u}_i - \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} \langle \vec{e}_j \mid \vec{u}_i \rangle \vec{e}_j $$
   and normalize:
   $$ \vec{e}_i = \frac{\vec{w}_i}{\sqrt{\langle \vec{w}_i \mid \vec{w}_i \rangle}} $$

* Unitary Operators and Unitary Representations

** Adjoint and Unitary Operators
- *Adjoint Operator $A^\dagger$:* The unique operator satisfying:
  $$ \langle \vec{v} \mid A^\dagger \vec{w} \rangle = \langle A \vec{v} \mid \vec{w} \rangle, \quad \forall \vec{v}, \vec{w} \in V $$
  In an orthonormal basis, $(A^\dagger)_{ij} = (A_{ji})^*$ (conjugate transpose).
- *Unitary Operator $U$:* An operator preserving the scalar product:
  $$ \langle U\vec{v} \mid U\vec{w} \rangle = \langle \vec{v} \mid \vec{w} \rangle \iff U^\dagger U = \mathbf{1}_V $$
  The set of unitary operators forms the group $\mathrm{Unit}(V) \cong \mathrm{U}(n)$, a subgroup of $\mathrm{Aut}(V) \cong \mathrm{GL}(n, \mathbb{C})$.

** Unitary Representations
A *unitary representation* of a group $G$ in an inner product space $V$ is a homomorphism:
$$ D : G \to \mathrm{Unit}(V), \quad g \mapsto D(g) $$
satisfying $D(g)^\dagger = D(g)^{-1} = D(g^{-1})$.
- *Unitary Equivalence:* Two unitary representations $U_1$ and $U_2$ are *unitarily equivalent* if there exists an isometric isomorphism $A$ ($A^\dagger A = \mathbf{1}$) such that:
  $$ U_2(g) = A U_1(g) A^{-1} = A U_1(g) A^\dagger, \quad \forall g \in G $$
Every $n$-dimensional unitary representation is unitarily equivalent to a homomorphism $G \to \mathrm{U}(n)$.

#+begin_important
*The Central Goal of Representation Theory in Physics:*
To classify all unitary representations of a physical symmetry group up to unitary equivalence.
#+end_important
